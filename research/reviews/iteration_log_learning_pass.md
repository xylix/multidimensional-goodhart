# Iteration-log learning pass

Status after consolidation: durable meta-diagnostic, not a current
theorem/application/policy gate. Use it for the mistake taxonomy and
stale-phrase scan discipline; use the current license map for live claims.

Question: what mistakes did we make because we were not yet applying the
response-modeling paradigm, and what information would we have received earlier
if we had used it?

This is a writing pass on the iteration log, not a new research iteration. The
"paradigm" here means the discipline that emerged by Iteration 14 and later:
before using a Goodhart claim, declare the type/action boundary, response
channel, action or search geometry, aggregation rule, hidden harm/value object,
evidence standard, and falsifier. Then test the claim at the cheapest layer that
can kill it.

## Main Learning

The early mistake was not mathematical carelessness in the narrow sense. Many
derivations were correct. The mistake was letting a correct calculation stand
too close to a slogan before the licensed object had been named.

The paradigm would not have prevented all errors. It would have changed what
the work asked for up front. Instead of "does adding a dimension conserve harm?"
it would have asked "which harm object, under which aggregation rule, for which
response channel, with which exchange rates, and what observation would make
this false?" That would have surfaced several missing variables before the
adversarial passes had to recover them.

## Findings

> "dimensional scaling requires assumptions about how `||r||_2` grows with the number of hidden dimensions. The correct harm functional remains unsettled."

Problem: The log already knew the calculation did not answer the motivating
dimensional claim, but it treated the missing harm functional as an open
follow-up rather than a gate on interpretation.

Revision/action: Future entries should add a "licensed object" line before the
result summary: "This licenses drift of declared coordinates under declared
coupling `r`; it does not license harm scaling until a value/harm functional is
declared."

Counterfactual information: We would have asked immediately whether hidden
coordinates have equal value, correlated value, or arbitrary value weights. That
would have brought the Iteration 15 value-weighted/operator version into view
earlier.

> "selection channels (`mu_theta << mu_0`; contains all of iterations 1-3) and intervention channels (`mu_theta` can be singular w.r.t. `mu_0`)."

Problem: The criterion used an observed-law relation where the later paradigm
needs a causal type/action relation. Absolute continuity is evidence about
support, not the boundary between reweighting and fixed-type response.

Revision/action: Rewrite the lesson as: "Selection changes weights over fixed
type-conditional behavior; intervention changes behavior at fixed type.
Absolute continuity and singularity are support diagnostics, not the causal
definition."

Counterfactual information: We would have asked what counts as type, what counts
as action, and what data distinguish reweighting from behavior change. That
would have exposed epsilon-baseline gaming and marginal non-identifiability
before Iterations 11-12.

> "total harm `H = t`, invariant to `M` -- closing a gamed channel only re-routes the fixed total; expanding `M` raises `K_M`, lowers the cheapest gaming cost, recruits more gamers (backfire)."

Problem: The sentence mixes three objects: fixed-deficit per-agent harm,
population entry, and metric-design advice. The algebra was narrow; the
headline was broad.

Revision/action: Replace the headline form with: "For fixed deficit and
equal-harm-per-score additive channels, `H_per(M,d)=d`. Adding an independently
gameable channel can increase `H_pop` by expanding the profitable gaming band."

Counterfactual information: The paradigm would have demanded `H_per` versus
`H_pop`, score weights `w_j`, harm rates `h_j`, and the population distribution
`F_Q` before the conclusion. Iterations 6, 17, and 21 recovered exactly that
missing information.

> "conservation under re-routing holds iff channels are equally harmful per score unit (`h_j = c w_j`)."

Problem: This is the moment the paradigm starts paying rent. The repair does
not merely add a caveat; it identifies the exchange-rate variable that decides
when the slogan is true.

Revision/action: Preserve this as a model of good log writing: state the
calculation, the iff-condition, and the object it applies to.

Counterfactual information: If this had been the Iteration 5 entry, we would
have learned sooner that the real claim was not "conservation Goodhart" but
"exchange-rate discipline for additive scorecards."

> "proxy optimization pressure induces a minimum-complexity hidden-drift attractor."

Problem: The claim used one word, "complexity," to cover private cost, search
accessibility, support size, rank, description length, and legibility. That is
abstract drift: the term named a vibe, not a measurable object.

Revision/action: Do not ask this question again without a predeclared
complexity functional and response mechanism. The better question is: "Under
this cost/search geometry, what residual shape is predicted?"

Counterfactual information: The paradigm would have asked for the response
geometry and the falsifier first. We would have seen at once that quadratic
costs can produce dense drift, fixed charges can produce lumpy sparse drift,
and simplicity-biased search needs a coding prior.

> "response-geometry attractor" is safe but too broad unless it preserves geometry-to-shape predictions.

Problem: This is a correct repair, but it shows the danger of replacing one
slogan with a broader, safer slogan. "Geometry matters" is true and nearly
empty unless it predicts a shape or blocks a transfer.

Revision/action: Every "response geometry" entry should name one predicted
contrast: dense/interior, active-face, lumpy fixed-charge, low-rank affordance,
or coding-prior simplicity.

Counterfactual information: We would have received earlier row-level tests
rather than a generic safety correction. Iterations 9-10 and 21 supplied that
information later.

> "The intervention envelope is `||h_u(a^*)|| <= sup_{a in B_u(V)} ||h_u(a)||`."

Problem: This risks making private affordability look like welfare control.
The hidden-harm map is present, but the value unit is still doing too much work
silently.

Revision/action: Pair any affordability bound with a declared welfare problem:
`W_ell(d,V) = sup { ell(a): c(a) <= V, w . a >= d }`, and distinguish it from
realized harm under a cost-minimizing response.

Counterfactual information: The paradigm would have forced the hidden value
weights before treating `m(d)` as useful. Iteration 19's counterexample
`w=(1,1)`, `h=(M,0)` shows what was missing.

> "A Goodhart claim must declare type space `U`, baseline kernel `K_0`, policy exposure `theta`, selection weights `W_theta` and/or response kernels `K_theta`, action/cost/search geometry, proxy/target relation, aggregation rule, hidden welfare/harm model, and evidence standard."

Problem: This is the real pivot. The prose is a little list-heavy, but the
thinking becomes cleaner after this point because claims start carrying their
own transfer conditions.

Revision/action: Keep the list, but in future compact logs turn it into a
checklist with a failure field: "Contract field missing or unavailable: ...;
consequence: no theorem transfer / no welfare credit / no diagnostic use."

Counterfactual information: This would have prevented several cycles where a
claim survived mathematically but later needed a separate audit to say what it
did not license.

> "MMLU alone does not supply them."

Problem: This is a good plain sentence. It names the absent information instead
of pretending the application proves or refutes the framework.

Revision/action: Use this pattern for every application: "[domain object] alone
does not supply [response primitives]."

Counterfactual information: Earlier application mapping would have asked for
finetuning data, benchmark access, search budget, reward loop, compute, KL
penalty, contamination traces, or same-checkpoint evidence before importing
Stackelberg or convex-cost language.

> "The live risk is post-hoc flexibility in `U`, `h`, `gamma`, `kappa`, and `V`."

Problem: This is the application version of the original math mistake. A
framework that can assign primitives after the fact can classify any story and
discipline none.

Revision/action: Require each application to mark primitives as predeclared,
audited, bounded, unavailable, or post-hoc. Post-hoc primitives should block
diagnostic calculations and hidden-target credit.

Counterfactual information: We would have learned earlier which application
claims change a real audit/design/evidence decision and which merely redescribe
known Goodhart stories.

> "broad policy prose is blocked."

Problem: This is a successful late-stage use of the paradigm. It turns missing
stochastic, empirical, and endogenous-stakes primitives into a stop sign, not a
caveat paragraph.

Revision/action: Preserve the gate. Future policy-facing entries should have
only two outcomes: "licensed narrow action" or "blocked until these primitives
exist."

Counterfactual information: The paradigm would have prevented toy results about
adaptive hardening from drifting into general regulator advice. Iterations
35-38 show the missing information: observation model, repair budget, stakes
update, floors, changing measured sets, and empirical estimability.

> "staleness risk in tracking documents."

Problem: Once the paradigm exists, the new failure mode is bureaucratic:
research notes, roadmaps, book prose, and paper prose can disagree about what
has been killed, narrowed, or promoted.

Revision/action: Add an explicit "stale phrase scan" to consolidation passes,
especially for old slogans: "dimension count," "minimum-complexity,"
"conservation," "policy," "optimal," "persistent cycle," and "endogenous V."

Counterfactual information: We would catch outdated framing before manuscript
prose inherits it.

## Mistake Taxonomy

1. Correct formula, wrong licensed object.
   Example: selection drift and additive conservation were algebraically useful
   before the value/harm object was named.

2. Observed distribution used as causal boundary.
   Example: absolute continuity was too weak and too strong in the wrong ways;
   the causal object is fixed-type response.

3. Slogan before exchange rates.
   Example: "conservation Goodhart" and "more metrics backfire" needed `w_j`,
   `h_j`, aggregation, and population entry.

4. Abstract noun before measurement.
   Example: "complexity" hid several incompatible shape notions.

5. Safe but empty repair.
   Example: "response geometry matters" only helps when it predicts a contrast
   or names a falsifier.

6. Application by analogy.
   Example: MMLU, hospitals, and scientific metrics needed primitives and
   discriminator observations before any toy calculation could travel.

7. Caveat treated as permission.
   Example: missing `kappa`, `h`, `gamma`, `w`, `V`, stochastic observation, or
   endogenous stakes must block claims, not merely soften them.

8. Audit-trail drift.
   Example: after a repair, stale open-question or manuscript wording can keep
   the old claim alive.

## Information the Paradigm Would Have Forced Earlier

- Which hidden value metric is being used, and whether Euclidean coordinates are
  part of the claim.
- Whether the mechanism is selection over fixed behavior or intervention at
  fixed type.
- Which object is conserved or harmed: coordinate drift, `H_per`, `H_pop`,
  realized `ell(a^*)`, or worst-case `W_ell`.
- The score weights, harm rates, aggregation rule, population distribution, and
  entry condition.
- The action/search geometry: quadratic, active-face, capped fixed-charge,
  low-rank affordance, search prior, or unavailable.
- The evidence needed to distinguish harmful gaming, harmless proxy repair, and
  real target improvement.
- Which primitives are predeclared, estimable, unavailable, or post-hoc.
- The exact falsifier or contract-failure condition.
- Whether a result licenses theorem prose, toy-example prose, application
  discipline, or no promotion.

## Proposed Future Log Template

Use this only for nontrivial future iterations.

```md
Question tackled:

Claim I might be tempted to use:

Contract:
- Type/action boundary:
- Channel: selection / intervention / mixture / unavailable
- Action or search geometry:
- Aggregation rule:
- Hidden value/harm object:
- Evidence standard or falsifier:
- Missing primitives and consequence:

What changed:

What the pass showed:

What this licenses:

What this does not license:

What's open now:

Red flags fired:
```

The important line is "Missing primitives and consequence." Without the
consequence, the paradigm becomes a decorated caveat list.

## Keep

> "The original Lagrange solution is still correct, but the headline was too broad."

Leave this pattern alone. It is honest, concrete, and teaches the reader how the
project learns.

> "MMLU alone does not supply them."

Leave this kind of sentence alone. It is short because it knows exactly what is
missing.

> "broad policy prose is blocked."

Leave this tone alone. It makes absence of license visible instead of burying it
under caution.
