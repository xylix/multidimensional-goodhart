# Priority-of-Discovery Investigation: The "Hidden-Harm Invariance" Theorem Is Not New

## TL;DR
- **The claimed result is a rediscovery.** Its proportionality condition hⱼ = c·wⱼ is verbatim the "perfect congruity" / "zero-distortion" condition of the accounting-economics performance-measurement literature (Feltham & Xie 1994; Baker 1992, 2002), in which the no-distortion condition is exactly that the performance-measure sensitivity vector be a positive scalar multiple of the value/objective sensitivity vector. The underlying optimization is the century-old equimarginal least-cost-allocation principle (Ramsey 1927; Baumol–Oates 1971; Montgomery 1972).
- **Mathematically, total harm to close deficit d is a weighted average of the channels' exchange rates hⱼ/wⱼ**, with weights κⱼwⱼ². (C) is the elementary fact that a weighted mean depends on the values being averaged and their weights, not on their count, and that adding a point pulls the mean toward that point (it can rise or fall). (D) is the elementary fact that a weighted mean over every subset equals the global value if and only if all the averaged quantities are equal — i.e. hⱼ/wⱼ = c, i.e. hⱼ = c·wⱼ. This "invariant across all subaggregates iff a proportionality condition holds" shape is the proportionality/equality test and consistency-in-aggregation theorem of index-number theory (Eichhorn–Voeller 1976; Blackorby–Primont 1980; Vartia 1976).
- **Single best candidate: Feltham & Xie (1994), with Baker (1992, 2002).** The proportionality condition is identical and the model (multi-task, separable quadratic effort cost, linear measure) is the same. (D)'s exact subset-invariance characterization is an *unstated one-line corollary* there, not a stated theorem, because that literature characterizes when proportionality yields *first-best/zero efficiency loss*, not when harm is *invariant to the measured subset*.

## Key Findings

**1. The structure reduces to a weighted mean of exchange rates.** With separable quadratic cost and a single linear deficit constraint, the cost-minimizing profile is aⱼ = λκⱼwⱼ (the equimarginal condition: marginal cost aⱼ/κⱼ equalized per unit of score weight, with λ = d/Σκⱼwⱼ²). Substituting, Harm = Σhⱼaⱼ = d·(Σκⱼwⱼhⱼ)/(Σκⱼwⱼ²), which is exactly d times a weighted arithmetic mean of rⱼ = hⱼ/wⱼ with weights κⱼwⱼ². Everything in (C) and (D) is a property of this one expression.

**2. (C) is a generic property of weighted means and is also the second-best sign-ambiguity.** Harm depends only on the exchange rates rⱼ and the cost-scaled weights κⱼwⱼ², never on the count of channels. Adding a channel adds one term to numerator and denominator — it moves the mean toward that channel's rⱼ, so harm rises if the new channel's rⱼ exceeds the incumbent weighted mean and falls otherwise. The direction-ambiguity of adding a margin is precisely the qualitative content of the Theory of the Second Best. Lipsey & Lancaster (1956), "The General Theory of Second Best," *Review of Economic Studies* 24(1):11–32, state it verbatim: "the removal of any one constraint may affect welfare or efficiency either by raising it, by lowering it, or by leaving it unchanged."

**3. (D)'s condition hⱼ = c·wⱼ is the congruity/zero-distortion condition, restated.** A weighted mean of {rⱼ} equals the same constant over *every* subset if and only if all rⱼ are equal, i.e. hⱼ/wⱼ = c for all j ⇔ h = c·w (harm-rate vector parallel to score-weight vector). In Baker (1992) this is proven as a necessary-and-sufficient condition for a first-best contract (P_e = V_e in all states of the world); in Baker (2002) it is the cosθ = 1 vertex of his distortion measure (1 − cosθ), where θ is the angle between the performance-measure gradient and the value gradient; in Feltham–Xie (1994) it is "perfect congruity," the sensitivity vector being proportional to the firm-value vector.

**4. The "invariant across all subsets iff proportional" form is index-number consistency-in-aggregation.** Index theory's proportionality/equality test (Eichhorn–Voeller 1976) states an index equals the common relative iff all relatives are equal; Blackorby–Primont (1980) prove that requiring two-stage = single-stage aggregation for *all* partitions forces a quasi-additive (proportional-weight) functional form. This is structurally identical to (D)'s universal-quantifier-over-subsets statement.

## Details

### Ranked candidates, closest first

**#1 — Feltham & Xie (1994), "Performance Measure Congruity and Diversity in Multi-Task Principal/Agent Relations," The Accounting Review 69(3):429–453; with Baker (1992), JPE 100(3):598–614, and Baker (2002), J. Human Resources 37(4):728–751.**
This is the home of the exact proportionality object. Feltham–Xie use precisely our setup: an action vector a, a linear value functional, linear contractible measures with a sensitivity matrix Y, and separable quadratic effort cost. As Budde (2007), "Performance Measure Congruity and the Balanced Scorecard," *Journal of Accounting Research* 45(3):515–539, states verbatim in reproducing their model, "V is assumed to be a linear function V(a) = d′a of the agent's action a, where d = (d₁,...,dₙ)′ is the vector of each activity's marginal product." The optimal aggregate measure has sensitivity y⁰ = Y(Y′Y)⁻¹Y′d, and first-best effort (no distortion) is achieved iff y⁰ ∝ d — for a single measure, iff its sensitivity vector is a scalar multiple of the value vector. They name this proportionality "congruity." Baker (1992) states the proportionality as an explicit iff for first-best; Baker (2002), p. 732, measures distortion as (1 − cosθ) with θ the angle between the performance-measure and value gradients, so zero distortion ⇔ cosθ = 1 ⇔ the two vectors are parallel. Datar–Kulp–Lambert (2001) and Budde (2007) reproduce the same congruity = squared-cosine (Rayleigh-quotient) object.

*Mapping.* Identify the agent's scored objective (the measure the agent responds to) with the score-weight vector w, and the designer's true concern (avoided harm) with the harm-rate vector h: performance-measure sensitivity ↦ w, value sensitivity ↦ h. Replace their equal-unit cost C(a)=½a′a with the channel-specific Σaⱼ²/(2κⱼ); this only changes the averaging weights from wⱼ² to κⱼwⱼ² and leaves the proportionality characterization untouched (any κⱼ > 0 gives the same h ∝ w condition). Then "perfect congruity / zero distortion" (h ∝ w) is exactly (D)'s condition hⱼ = c·wⱼ.

*Where it breaks.* (i) Feltham–Xie/Baker *maximize net surplus by choosing a piece rate b and trade distortion against a risk term*; our agent merely closes a fixed deficit at minimum cost with no contract design and no risk. Our problem is the risk-neutral, pure-effort-allocation degenerate case of their model. (ii) Their theorem characterizes proportionality ⇔ *first-best / zero efficiency loss*; it does **not** state proportionality ⇔ *harm invariant to the measured subset of channels*. In fact Budde (2007) emphasizes that the earlier congruity metrics "all... build on a specific cost function in which actions are independent and marginal costs are equal among tasks" and are "capable of identifying the lowest misallocation of effort only," and he studies how congruity *changes* as the model is generalized "to incorporate interactive actions" — the opposite emphasis to invariance, with congruity rising in, and depending on, the specific measure set. (iii) Baker's distortion is a scalar magnitude (the orthogonal gradient component), not a per-channel rate whose subset-average is examined. **Verdict: (D) is an unstated one-line corollary**, and the welfare interpretation differs (in their world h ∝ w means *no harm*; in (D) it means harm is *the same regardless of subset*, not necessarily low).

**#2 — Index-number consistency-in-aggregation / proportionality test (Eichhorn & Voeller 1976; Blackorby & Primont 1980, J. Econ. Theory 22:87–98; Vartia 1976).**
This is the home of (D)'s *shape*. "Outcome invariant across all subaggregates/partitions iff a proportionality condition holds" is the index-theory consensus condition: consistency-in-aggregation for all partitions forces proportional (quasi-additive) weights, and the equality/proportionality test says the index equals the common value iff all relatives are equal. *Where it breaks:* it concerns price/quantity indices, with no cost-minimizing agent, no fixed deficit, and no unpriced externality; the proportionality is over price relatives, not harm-to-score ratios. The correspondence is at the level of the abstract weighted-mean theorem, not the economic model.

**#3 — Equimarginal / least-cost allocation: Ramsey (1927); Baumol & Oates (1971); Montgomery (1972).**
This is the home of (C)'s optimization. To hit a target at least cost, equalize marginal cost across sources; the resulting minimized total is governed by the marginal rates, not by how many sources participate. The Ramsey rule equalizes the marginal deadweight loss (or proportional demand reduction) across taxed goods; least-cost abatement equalizes marginal abatement cost across an arbitrary number of sources. *Where it breaks:* in these models the thing being summed (deadweight loss, abatement cost) *is* the object being minimized and is priced/internalized; there is no second, hidden, unpriced harm vector h sitting outside the objective, and none of them states the subset-invariance iff.

**#4 — Theory of the Second Best (Lipsey & Lancaster 1956, Rev. Econ. Stud. 24(1):11–32).**
Explains the sign-ambiguity half of (C): introducing one more constraint/margin into an already-distorted optimization changes the remaining optimality conditions in a generally unsignable direction ("either by raising it, by lowering it, or by leaving it unchanged"). *Where it breaks:* purely qualitative; no proportionality condition, no closed-form harm.

**#5 — Holmström (1979) informativeness / Banker & Datar (1989) linear aggregation.**
Banker–Datar give optimal weights proportional to sensitivity × precision and discuss the value of adding a signal; Holmström's informativeness principle says an added signal has value iff it is not a sufficient statistic. Related in spirit ("when does an added channel matter"), but the object is the statistical value of information for incentive provision, not the invariance of an externality, and the proportionality is of optimal weights to sensitivity/noise, not of harm to score weight.

### Verdict (required form)
The closest prior art is **Feltham & Xie (1994), "Performance Measure Congruity and Diversity in Multi-Task Principal/Agent Relations," The Accounting Review 69(3):429–453, with Baker (1992, 2002)**. It establishes that, in a multi-task model with separable quadratic effort cost and linear performance measures, an agent's induced effort is undistorted (first-best) **if and only if the performance-measure sensitivity vector is proportional to the value-sensitivity vector** — Baker (2002, p. 732) measures the failure of this as distortion = 1 − cosθ between the two gradient vectors. **Result (D) is an unstated one-line corollary there**, because that literature characterizes proportionality as the condition for *zero efficiency loss / first-best*, and never states the distinct claim that hidden harm is *invariant to which subset of channels is measured* (indeed it emphasizes that congruity rises with, and depends on, the specific set of measures). To map it onto the claimed result, set the performance-measure sensitivity vector ↦ w (score weights), the value/objective sensitivity vector ↦ h (hidden-harm rates), and the cost matrix ↦ diag(1/κⱼ) (their equal-unit cost is the special case κⱼ ≡ 1). The correspondence holds exactly at the level of the proportionality condition (h ∝ w ⇔ hⱼ = c·wⱼ), and **breaks at the theorem's content**: they prove proportionality ⇔ first-best, whereas (D) proves proportionality ⇔ subset-invariance of harm — the same algebraic condition characterizing a different property, supplied by the elementary weighted-mean fact (a mean over every subset is constant iff the aggregand is constant), which is itself the proportionality/equality test of index-number theory.

No source in any searched field was found stating the **exact subset-invariance characterization** — "hidden harm to close a fixed deficit is invariant across all measured subsets of channels iff harm rate is proportional to score weight on the whole pool" — as a named theorem or explicit corollary. Specifically, searches on (a) "performance measure aggregation subset invariance," (b) "congruity invariant across subsets of measures," (c) Şabac & Yoo (2018), "Performance Measure Aggregation in Multi-Task Agencies," *Contemporary Accounting Research* 35(2):716–733, and (d) index-number proportionality applied to incentive distortion did not surface a verbatim statement of (D). The condition itself (h ∝ w) is ubiquitous; the *subset-invariance reading of it* appears to be unwritten in the non-AI literature, though it is a corollary a competent reader of Feltham–Xie/Baker plus index-number theory could write in one line.

## Recommendations
1. **Do not claim novelty for the condition hⱼ = c·wⱼ.** Cite it as the congruity (Feltham–Xie 1994) / zero-distortion (Baker 1992, 2002) condition: harm-rate vector parallel to score-weight vector. Any draft asserting this is new will be rejected by a referee from accounting theory or contract theory.
2. **Frame the genuine (if modest) contribution precisely.** The non-obvious step is *not* the proportionality condition but the *subset-invariance reading*: that proportionality is the iff condition for total harm to be invariant to **which and how many** channels are measured. Position this as a corollary connecting (i) equimarginal least-cost allocation, (ii) the congruity/distortion object, and (iii) the index-number proportionality/equality test — explicitly citing all three.
3. **Stage the priority claim defensively.** State (C) as following from the equimarginal principle and second-best sign-ambiguity; state (D)'s condition as congruity; reserve any originality claim for the explicit subset-invariance statement, and even there note it is a one-line corollary.
4. **Benchmark that would change the verdict to "fully anticipated":** locate, in Şabac–Yoo (2018), Christensen–Şabac–Tian (2010, "Ranking Performance Measures in Multi-Task Agencies," *The Accounting Review* 85(5):1545–1575), or Feltham–Hofmann–Indjejikian (2016), an explicit proposition that distortion/congruity is invariant to the measured subset iff global proportionality. If such a proposition exists, (D) is not merely an unstated corollary but a stated theorem, and no novelty remains. **Benchmark that would preserve a narrow novelty claim:** confirming (via full-text reads of those three papers) that all of them study how congruity *changes* with the measure set and none states the invariance iff.

## Caveats
- The exact proposition numbers in Feltham–Xie (1994) and Baker (2002) were reconstructed from faithful reproductions (Budde 2007; Datar–Kulp–Lambert 2001) and direct quotations, because the original published PDFs are paywalled; the proportionality condition and its iff status are confirmed (Baker 1992's necessity-and-sufficiency proof is in the original; Baker 2002's cosine definition is anchored to p. 732 by Budde 2007), but a reader should verify the original proposition labels.
- Feltham–Xie/Baker assume *equal* marginal effort costs across tasks (κⱼ ≡ 1); the claimed result's channel-specific κⱼ is a mild generalization that changes only the averaging weights (κⱼwⱼ²), not the proportionality characterization. This generalization is itself anticipated by Budde's cost-matrix extension and by the general equimarginal allocation.
- The interpretive inversion is essential and easy to get wrong: in the congruity/distortion literature h ∝ w characterizes the *best* case (no harm/distortion), whereas in (D) it characterizes *invariance* of harm to the subset (harm can be uniformly high). The shared algebra should not be mistaken for shared economic meaning.
- "Could move harm up or down" in (C) is established here by the weighted-mean argument and corroborated by the second-best literature's sign-ambiguity (Lipsey–Lancaster 1956); no source was found that states this specific monotonicity-failure for *hidden harm* as a named result.
