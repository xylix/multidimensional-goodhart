# Prior-Art Search: Least-Cost Allocation Against an Additive Score with an Unpriced Linear Externality

## TL;DR
- Results **(A)** the equimarginal allocation a_j ∝ κ_j w_j and **(B)** the harm formula H(d) = d·(Σ h_jκ_jw_j)/(Σ κ_jw_j²) are STANDARD and live, in isomorphic form, in two named bodies of work — least-cost pollution abatement (the equimarginal principle, Baumol–Oates tradition) and the quadratic-cost multitask principal–agent model (Holmström–Milgrom 1991; Feltham–Xie 1994; Baker 2002). You will not be able to claim these as novel.
- Result **(D)**, the invariance-iff-proportionality theorem, is at its mathematical core the multitask CONGRUENCE / no-distortion condition (Feltham–Xie 1994; Baker 2002: zero distortion iff the measure's sensitivity vector is proportional to the true-value vector), and its equal-weights special case is the Feldstein (1999)/Chetty (2009) "taxable-income elasticity is a sufficient statistic" condition. As an explicit *iff* theorem about invariance of required harm across ALL subsets of a measurement pool with arbitrary weights, it appears UNSTATED — a clean repackaging/rediscovery, not a known named result.
- Result **(C)**, set-monotonicity (adding a channel can move harm either way; the exchange rate h_j/w_j, not the dimension count, governs), is a corollary of "a new element pulls a weighted average toward itself"; its closest explicit prior statement is Datar–Kulp–Lambert (2001), with Lipsey–Lancaster's second-best theorem as the qualitative ancestor.

## Key Findings

**The optimization (A)+(B) is standard and has two named homes.** Minimizing Σ a_j²/(2κ_j) subject to Σ w_j a_j ≥ d gives the Lagrange condition a_j/κ_j = λw_j, i.e. a_j = d·κ_jw_j/(Σκ_jw_j²), and Harm = Σh_ja_j = d·(Σh_jκ_jw_j)/(Σκ_jw_j²). The decisive structural observation is that H(d)/d is a **κ_jw_j²-weighted average of the per-channel exchange rates h_j/w_j**. This is the same mathematics as (i) least-cost abatement across sources with different marginal-cost slopes, where the equimarginal principle equalizes marginal abatement cost and the "harm" is a co-pollutant/co-benefit incurred at a fixed rate per unit of abatement; and (ii) the quadratic-cost multitask agency model, where the agent equalizes marginal private cost per unit of incentive across tasks.

**(D) is the congruence condition in disguise.** If h_j = c·w_j for all j in the pool, then Harm = Σ(c·w_j)a_j = c·Σw_ja_j = c·d — the harm is pinned by the deficit alone, independent of the allocation and therefore of which channels are available/measured. Conversely, since H/d is a weighted average of the exchange rates h_j/w_j, that average is invariant to the subset taken iff all exchange rates are equal, i.e. h_j/w_j = c. This is exactly the multitask congruence condition (Feltham–Xie 1994; Baker 2002): no distortion iff the measure's sensitivity vector is proportional to the true-value vector. The packaging as "invariance to the measured subset" with the universal-subset quantifier is what appears new.

**(C) is the weighted-average-pull fact, with a named agency precedent.** Adding channel k pulls H/d toward h_k/w_k, so total harm rises if h_k/w_k exceeds the incumbent weighted average and falls otherwise; the count of channels is not the governing variable. Datar–Kulp–Lambert (2001) prove the closest explicit comparative static of this either-direction type.

## Details — Ranked Shortlist (closest first)

### 1. Chetty (2009), "Is the Taxable Income Elasticity Sufficient to Calculate Deadweight Loss? The Implications of Evasion and Avoidance" — AEJ: Economic Policy 1(2):31–52 (public finance) — closest for B and D
- **Correspondence:** channels = behavioral margins (labor supply l, sheltering e); deficit d = the targeted reduction in tax liability; the agent equates the marginal PRIVATE cost of each margin to the tax rate t (the equimarginal condition — A); w_j ≈ 1 (each dollar of taxable-income reduction reduces revenue equally); h_j = the marginal RESOURCE/social cost of margin j (the unpriced harm); κ_j = inverse curvature of each margin's cost.
- **Contains:** A — the agent equates the marginal cost of every behavior to t (Chetty, on the canonical case: "an optimizing agent equates the marginal cost of sheltering $1 of income from taxation with the net marginal cost of reducing earnings by $1, so the reason that reported taxable income falls does not matter for efficiency calculations"). B explicitly, in the generalized case — Chetty's abstract states: **"excess burden depends on a weighted average of the taxable income and total earned income elasticities, with the weight determined by the marginal resource cost of sheltering income from taxation."** D in its equal-weights special case: Feldstein's (1999) sufficient-statistic result holds iff the marginal social cost of every margin equals t — a no-wedge / proportionality condition. Chetty's own framing: Feldstein's formula "implicitly requires that the marginal social cost of sheltering $1 of income equals the tax rate," and in the pure-transfer case sheltering "does not lead to deadweight loss because the cost z(e,t) for the agent is exactly offset by the positive fiscal externality z(e,t) on government revenue," which "completely sever[s] the link between the taxable income elasticity and excess burden." When the wedge differs across margins, composition matters — the C-flavored point.
- **Diverges:** weights are uniform (w_j ≡ 1), so the full "h_j = c·w_j" generalization collapses to "h_j = c (same across margins)"; framed as sufficiency of a statistic, not as invariance across all subsets of a pool; no explicit "adding a margin moves harm either way" theorem; the externality is partly priced (transfers to government/other agents).

### 2. Feltham–Xie (1994) + Baker (2002) + Datar–Kulp–Lambert (2001) — multitask agency — closest for A, the proportionality form of D, and C
- **Feltham–Xie 1994** (The Accounting Review 69(3):429–453): value V(a)=d′a; measures P=Y′a+ε; separable quadratic cost C(a)=½a′a. Optimal contract s⁰=(Y′Y)⁻¹Y′d; second-best effort a⁰=Ys⁰; second-best profit ½·d′Y(Y′Y)⁻¹Y′d (p. 433). **Congruence (zero distortion) iff the achievable sensitivity vector y⁰ is proportional to the value vector d.** Schnedler's Definition of congruence makes the proportionality explicit: "Performance measure and benefit are congruent if their coefficient vectors have the same direction: b = γβ for some γ different from zero," and he notes this benchmark is shared across Feltham–Xie (1994), Feltham–Wu (2000), Baker (2000, 2002), and Datar et al. (2001).
- **Baker 2002** (J. Human Resources 37(4):728–751): defines congruity as the cosine of the angle between value vector d and measure-sensitivity vector y; surplus = (cos(d,y))²·Π^FB; zero distortion iff d and y are parallel. (Budde 2007 confirms the attribution: "I refer to Baker [2000, 2002], who proposes the cosine of d and y as a metric of congruity for a single performance measure with sensitivities y.") This is precisely the proportionality form of D.
- **Datar–Kulp–Lambert 2001** (J. Accounting Research 39(1):75–92): the closest explicit either-direction comparative static (C). Verbatim: **"an increase in the sensitivity of a performance measure to an agent's action does not necessarily increase the weight placed on that performance measure, even if that measure is perfectly congruent with the firm's outcome."**
- **Correspondence:** tasks = channels; incentive weights ≈ w_j; quadratic effort cost ½a′a (κ_j = 1, or a′Ca generally); the agent's IC first-order condition gives effort = (cost-inverse)×(weight·sensitivity), i.e. a=Y′s when C=I and e*=(w₁/2)C⁻¹b generally (A); "harm" = distortion = loss of true value from misaligned effort; h ∝ w corresponds to sensitivity ∝ value.
- **Contains:** A (equimarginal effort, exact); D's proportionality condition (congruence ⇔ sensitivity ∝ value, proven/definitional); C (DKL non-monotonicity). Budde (2007) formalizes a "balance" condition under which first-best (zero distortion) is attainable regardless of which balanced measure set is used — the closest existing thing to D's subset-invariance, but stated as first-best-attainability, not as harm-invariance across all subsets.
- **Diverges:** the object is unconstrained surplus-maximization with risk, not a least-cost problem hitting a fixed deficit d; harm is the internalized loss of the principal's value, not a third party's externality; cost is typically equal-weight quadratic (κ_j equal). Schnedler (2008) shows the clean congruence result is special to C = cI (separable equal costs); for general a′Ca the congruent measure is no longer optimal. No paper states that harm-to-close-a-deficit is invariant across ALL subsets iff h∝w.

### 3. Least-cost abatement / equimarginal principle (environmental economics & OR; Baumol–Oates tradition) + co-pollutant literature — closest for the A/B mechanics
- **Correspondence:** channels = abatement at different sources/technologies; deficit d = mandated total emission reduction; cost = source-specific convex/quadratic abatement cost (κ_j = inverse MAC slope); the equimarginal principle equalizes marginal abatement cost (A); h_j = ancillary harm/co-benefit per unit of abatement at source j (the unpriced externality, NOT in the cost-minimizer's objective); H/d = weighted average of co-harm rates.
- **Contains:** A explicitly — the equimarginal principle is the textbook least-cost-allocation result ("allocate abatement among multiple sources such that marginal costs of abatement are equal"); B implicitly when costs are quadratic. The co-pollutant/co-benefit literature (e.g. Muller 2012; multipollutant cost-efficiency studies) treats per-unit ancillary harms and "trade ratios" — i.e. exchange rates between dissimilar pollutants.
- **Diverges:** the co-harm is usually eventually priced or co-optimized; the literature does not state the invariance-iff-proportionality theorem (D) or the set-monotonicity static (C) about WHICH sources are in the regulated set; focus is cost, with harm a secondary "co-benefit" rather than the object of a conservation theorem.

### 4. Feldstein (1999) / Slemrod–Yitzhaki — the sufficient-statistic invariance — supporting D
- Feldstein's claim that the taxable-income elasticity summarizes deadweight loss "regardless of whether its origin is responsiveness of labor supply or some other behavior" is precisely an invariance-to-composition statement; it holds under the no-wedge (proportionality) condition. This is the conceptual root of D in public finance, predating Chetty's generalization. Slemrod and Yitzhaki remark that "Feldstein's (1999) claim about the central importance of the elasticity of taxable income generalizes to avoidance and evasion" — the composition-invariance intuition stated informally.

### 5. Theory of the second best (Lipsey–Lancaster 1956, Review of Economic Studies 24(1):11–32) — conceptual ancestor of C
- Establishes that adding/removing one distortion while others remain can move welfare in either direction. Verbatim: **"in general, nothing can be said about the direction or the magnitude of the secondary departures from optimum conditions made necessary by the original non-fulfillment of one condition."** This is the qualitative ancestor of set-monotonicity (C) but contains no closed-form weighted-average formula and no proportionality invariance condition.

### 6. Mean-variance / tracking-error portfolio optimization with an unpriced factor — structural twin for A/B
- Minimizing quadratic (tracking-error) risk subject to a target active return, with exposure to an unpriced/misaligned risk factor playing the role of h_j. The MOSEK Portfolio Optimization Cookbook notes that misalignment between risk and alpha factor models means "the optimizer will interpret such misalignments as factor return without risk or factor risk without return and it will exploit them as false opportunities." Same KKT structure as A; harm = exposure to the unpriced factor. No D/C theorem; the factor is usually constrained or priced.

### 7. Reynaert–Sallee (2016), "Corrective Policy and Goodhart's Law: The Case of Carbon Emissions from Automobiles" — NBER WP 22911 (environmental economics) — adjacent, an explicitly NON-ML "Goodhart's Law for externalities"
- A formal "Goodhart's Law for externality-correcting policies": stronger policy → more gaming; gaming has resource costs and choice distortions. Their **Proposition 4** gives an either-direction comparative static: "As long as h′/D is rising in [the signal]…" gaming rises with policy stringency, but "if a tighter policy causes demand to shrink very rapidly … there is less gaming under a tighter standard," with the clean case "Goodhart's Law holds unambiguously (dg/dλ > 0) when gaming is a marginal cost instead of a fixed cost, or if demand is held constant." This is a monopolist/single-attribute pricing-and-gaming model, not the multi-channel additive-score allocation; it does not contain A–D, but it is the closest *named, non-AI* "Goodhart" formalization in the externality literature.

### 8. Composite-indicator theory (OECD/JRC 2008 Handbook; Atkinson/Sen tradition) and target-gaming in public management (Bevan & Hood 2006) — informal only
- The OECD/JRC Handbook on Constructing Composite Indicators debates whether adding indicators (with correlation/double-counting — "if two collinear indicators are included … the unique dimension … would have weight (w1+w2)") improves or degrades a weighted index, and treats weighting/aggregation axioms — but the discussion is methodological, not a theorem; no closed-form harm formula, no invariance condition. Bevan & Hood (2006, Public Administration 84(3):517–538), the "synecdoche" / "what's measured is what matters" paper, is a case-study/typology of gaming, not a formal model.

Present your H(d) formula as the closed form of "harm = a κ_jw_j²-weighted average of exchange rates h_j/w_j."
2. **Position D as a repackaging of the congruence condition.** State explicitly that "h_j = c·w_j" is the abatement/agency analog of Feltham–Xie/Baker congruence (sensitivity ∝ value) and of the Feldstein/Chetty no-wedge condition. Your contribution is the SUBSET-INVARIANCE framing ("invariant across all subsets of the pool iff proportional") with arbitrary weights, which the prior statements do not make with the universal-subset quantifier. To defend novelty rigorously, verify the exact wording of the Feltham–Xie propositions and Budde (2007)'s "balance" proposition against the paywalled originals.
3. **Position C against Datar–Kulp–Lambert (2001) and second-best.** Cite DKL for the proven either-direction weight result and Lipsey–Lancaster for the qualitative ancestor; claim as your own the clean, closed-form statement "dimension count is not the governing variable; the exchange rate h_j/w_j is."
4. **Benchmark that would change the verdict:** if a direct reading of Feltham–Xie (1994), Datar–Kulp–Lambert (2001), or Chetty (2009) reveals an explicit "invariant to the included set iff proportional" proposition with arbitrary (non-uniform) weights, downgrade D from "apparently unstated" to "special case of a known result." ## Verdict
Results (A) and (B) are standard and have two clean named homes — the equimarginal principle of least-cost pollution abatement and the quadratic-cost multitask principal–agent model. Result (C) — adding a measured dimension can move total harm in either direction, with the exchange rate, not the count, governing — is present in essentially the form you want in Datar–Kulp–Lambert (2001) ("an increase in the sensitivity of a performance measure … does not necessarily increase the weight placed on that performance measure, even if that measure is perfectly congruent") and has a qualitative ancestor in Lipsey–Lancaster's second-best theorem. Result (D) — harm-to-close-a-deficit invariant to the measured subset iff harm rate is proportional to score weight — is, at its mathematical core, the multitask CONGRUENCE/no-distortion condition of Feltham–Xie (1994) and Baker (2002) (zero distortion iff the measure's sensitivity vector is proportional to the true-value vector), and its equal-weights special case is Feldstein (1999)/Chetty (2009)'s "the taxable-income elasticity is a sufficient statistic for deadweight loss iff the marginal social cost equals the tax rate on every margin." But the SPECIFIC packaging — an *iff* theorem about invariance of required harm across ALL subsets of a measurement pool with arbitrary weights — appears UNSTATED in any prior field; it is best characterized as a rediscovery/repackaging that unifies the agency congruence condition with the abatement equimarginal allocation. **The single closest prior statement is Chetty (2009)'s weighted-average excess-burden formula together with the Feldstein/Chetty no-wedge (sufficiency) condition for the B+D package, with Feltham–Xie (1994)/Baker (2002) congruence the closest statement of D's proportionality condition itself.**
