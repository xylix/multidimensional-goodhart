# Citation Audit: Does the AI-Safety / Formal-Goodhart Literature Bridge to the Performance-Measurement and χ²-Estimation Prior Art?

## TL;DR
- **The gap is largely REAL but not total.** After exhausting the prescribed searches, the strongest refuting bridges are *economics-side* (congruity / multitask) bridges in two recent AI papers — Haupt et al. 2026 (benchmark aggregation) and Wang & Huang 2026 (distortion index) — that genuinely tie Holmström–Milgrom 1991 / Baker 2002 to Goodhart in AI. **No paper bridges the χ²-DRO / Chapman–Robbins estimation branch to Goodhart;** that branch's gap is fully intact.
- **Per branch:** congruity = gap PARTIALLY CLOSED (HM1991 + Baker reached; Feltham–Xie / Banker–Datar / Datar–Kulp–Lambert / Bénabou–Tirole NOT reached as Goodhart math); sufficiency/informativeness = gap REAL; χ²-DRO / Chapman–Robbins = gap FULLY REAL (zero type-(i) bridges); corrective-Goodhart (Reynaert–Sallee) = gap REAL in the formal corpus.
- **New exceptions found beyond the four known:** Haupt, Hartenstein, Reuel, Kochenderfer & Koyejo 2026 ("Welfare, Improvability, and Variance," arXiv 2605.30916) — a type-(i) congruity bridge; and Hadfield-Menell & Hadfield "Incomplete Contracting and AI Alignment" — a strong type-(ii)/borderline-(i) multitask bridge. The χ²-DRO branch produced only type-(ii) citations (DARC, χPO).

## Key Findings

1. **The single strongest refuting citation is Haupt et al. 2026** ("Welfare, Improvability, and Variance: A Principal–Agent Approach to Optimal Benchmark Item Aggregation," arXiv 2605.30916, Stanford). It explicitly adapts the multitask principal–agent framework of Holmström–Milgrom 1991 to AI benchmark design, names the congruity literature's "distortion," and frames the whole problem as the "canonical Goodhart/Campbell pathology—once a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure." This is a genuine **type-(i)** congruity-branch bridge — but it reaches HM1991 and Baker 2002, not Feltham–Xie 1994, Banker–Datar 1989, Datar–Kulp–Lambert 2001, or Bénabou–Tirole 2016 as Goodhart mathematics.

2. **The χ²-DRO / Chapman–Robbins branch has ZERO type-(i) bridges.** Despite χ²-divergence being heavily used in reward-overoptimization work, no reward-hacking paper cites the Hammersley–Chapman–Robbins bound, Ben-Tal et al. 2013, or Namkoong–Duchi 2017 as the math of Goodhart. This is striking because the HCR bound *literally is* the χ²-divergence variance bound: per Wikipedia's statement, the bound was "independently discovered by John Hammersley in 1950, and by Douglas Chapman and Herbert Robbins in 1951," and is derived via "the variational representation of chi-squared divergence," yielding Var_θ[ĝ] ≥ sup_{θ'≠θ} (E_{θ'}[ĝ]−E_θ[ĝ])² / χ²(μ_{θ'};μ_θ) — exactly the proxy-vs-true error structure of Goodhart.

3. **The closest χ²-branch citation is type-(ii):** the χPO paper (Audrey Huang, Wenhao Zhan, Tengyang Xie, Jason D. Lee, Wen Sun, Akshay Krishnamurthy & Dylan J. Foster, "Correcting the Mythos of KL-Regularization," arXiv 2407.13399, ICLR 2025) — "the first practical and general-purpose offline alignment algorithm that is provably robust to overoptimization" — cites only "Duchi and Namkoong 2019," and solely for the genealogy claim that "χ²-divergence is known to play a fundamental role in asymptotic statistics." That is a tooling/genealogy cite, not a Goodhart bridge. DARC (a risk-constrained decoding paper, arXiv 2603.08145) cites Namkoong–Duchi for distributionally-robust *decoding* tooling, again type-(ii).

4. **Wang & Huang 2026** ("Reward Hacking as Equilibrium under Finite Evaluation," arXiv 2603.28063) is confirmed as a type-(i) bridge that instantiates HM1991 + Baker 1992 into a formal "distortion index" tied to Goodhart — but, as the known-exceptions list anticipated, it reaches only those two items and re-derives the rest, citing no χ²/estimation prior art.

## Details

### Branch 1 — Congruity (Feltham–Xie 1994, Banker–Datar 1989, Baker 1992/2002 distortion = 1−cosθ, Datar–Kulp–Lambert 2001, Bénabou–Tirole 2016)

**Verdict: gap PARTIALLY CLOSED.** Two type-(i) bridges exist, but both reach only the Holmström–Milgrom 1991 / Baker 2002 nodes, not the deeper congruity-geometry items.

- **Haupt et al. 2026 (arXiv 2605.30916) — TYPE (i), strongest bridge.** Citing sentence (verbatim): "We close this gap by adapting the multitask principal–agent framework of Holmstrom and Milgrom (1991)—in which a designer weighting noisy measures of effort faces an effort-substitution problem when measures vary in distortion and precision—to benchmark design." It explicitly names the Goodhart framing: "It documents the canonical Goodhart/Campbell pathology—once a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure—... The lessons generalize known multitask-distortion findings from manager incentives (Baker, 2002), teaching-to-the-test (Jacob and Levitt, 2003), healthcare pay-for-performance (Campbell et al., 2007), and policing statistics (Eterno and Silverman, 2012): a single scalar index, applied to a multidimensional capability, predictably reallocates effort toward what is measured and away from what is valued." This connects multitask-distortion math to a Goodhart-framed AI measurement problem. **However:** its bibliography reaches Holmström–Milgrom 1987/1991 and Baker 2002 — it does NOT cite Feltham–Xie 1994, Banker–Datar 1989, Datar–Kulp–Lambert 2001, or Bénabou–Tirole 2016 as the operative mathematics. The Baker cosine-distortion (1−cosθ) congruity index itself is not invoked.

- **Wang & Huang 2026 (arXiv 2603.28063) — TYPE (i), known exception confirmed.** Per retrieval: "Our framework instantiates the multi-task principal-agent model of Holmström and Milgrom (1991) in the AI alignment setting … to derive a computable distortion index Dᵢ that predicts both the direction and severity of hacking." Cites Holmström–Milgrom 1991 and Baker 1992 explicitly. Again reaches only those two items, re-deriving the rest from moral-hazard comparative statics rather than the congruity-geometry literature.

- **Hadfield-Menell & Hadfield, "Incomplete Contracting and AI Alignment" — TYPE (ii)/borderline (i).** Uses Holmström–Milgrom 1991 multitasking and Baker et al. 1994 to argue AI reward incompleteness is a multi-tasking problem: "The lesson of Holmstrom & Milgrom (1991) for AI is that a singular focus on improving performance on the measurable task may degrade performance on the unmeasurable." This is the multitask-distortion intuition applied to alignment, but framed as incomplete-contracting/agency rather than as a formal Goodhart distortion theorem — closer to type-(ii).

- The deep congruity items (Feltham–Xie, Banker–Datar, Datar–Kulp–Lambert, Baker's cosine index) are discussed almost exclusively *within the accounting/economics literature itself* (Budde working papers; Springer "Economics of Accounting"), never inside the AI-safety reward-hacking corpus as Goodhart mathematics.

### Branch 2 — Sufficiency / Informativeness (Holmström 1979 informativeness principle, sufficient statistic)

**Verdict: gap REAL.** The informativeness principle (Holmström 1979) and the sufficient-statistic result are cited in the broader strategic-ML / mechanism-design literature (e.g., "Manipulation-Proof Machine Learning" cites Holmström 1979 alongside Hardt et al. 2016), but that is the strategic-classification lineage, not the reward-hacking / formal-Goodhart corpus, so per the rules of engagement it does not refute. Within AI-safety, Holmström 1979 appears in control/agency-framed work (e.g., "AI Testing Should Account for Sophisticated Strategic Behaviour" cites Holmström 1979 moral hazard) but not as the informativeness / sufficient-statistic characterization of the proxy-vs-true-reward gap. No type-(i) bridge found.

### Branch 3 — χ²-DRO / Estimation (Hammersley–Chapman–Robbins, Ben-Tal et al. 2013, Namkoong–Duchi 2017)

**Verdict: gap FULLY REAL — the hardest-pressed branch yielded zero type-(i) bridges.**

| Estimation item | In reward-hacking corpus? | Bridge type |
|---|---|---|
| Hammersley–Chapman–Robbins bound | No citation anywhere (only in an unrelated differential-privacy paper) | none |
| Ben-Tal et al. 2013 | No citation in any reward-hacking paper; only generic DRO reviews | none |
| Namkoong–Duchi 2017 / Duchi–Namkoong 2019 | Cited only as generic χ²-DRO / variance-regularization tooling | type-(ii) only |

- **χPO (Huang et al. 2024, arXiv 2407.13399)** uses χ²-divergence explicitly against overoptimization, but its only nod to the estimation lineage is a footnote: "More classically, χ²-divergence is known to play a fundamental role in asymptotic statistics (Tsybakov, 2008; Duchi and Namkoong, 2019)." This is type-(ii) (genealogy/tooling), not a Goodhart bridge. It does NOT cite Chapman–Robbins, Ben-Tal 2013, or Namkoong–Duchi 2017.
- **Laidlaw, Singhal & Dragan 2024 (arXiv 2403.03185; "Correlated Proxies," ICLR 2025, v4)** frames occupancy-measure / χ²-divergence regularization explicitly as Goodhart mitigation — "our theory suggests regularizing the χ² divergence between the policies' occupancy measures can be more effective" than the KL penalty — but cites the imitation-learning / offline-RL lineage (Ho & Ermon 2016, Hazan et al. 2019), NOT the HCR / Ben-Tal / Namkoong–Duchi estimation lineage. This is the single most important negative, confirmed by checking the actual reference list and introduction.
- **Liu, Sun & Zheng 2026 ("Robust Optimization for Mitigating Reward Hacking with Correlated Proxies," arXiv 2604.12086)** uses a χ²-over-occupancy-measure max-min DRO formulation framed as reward-hacking, but its DRO citations are Hu & Hong 2013, Lam 2016, Delage & Ye 2010, Goh & Sim 2010 — none of the three target items. Type-(ii).
- **DARC (arXiv 2603.08145)** is the closest: it cites Namkoong & Duchi 2016 and Duchi & Namkoong 2019 and ties χ²-DRO to "proxy over-optimization," but routes it through the distributionally-robust *decoding* role, not as a variance lower bound on the true-reward error. Type-(ii).
- **Kwa, Thomas & Garriga-Alonso 2024 "Catastrophic Goodhart" (arXiv 2407.14503, NeurIPS 2024)** proves a heavy-tailed-error Goodhart result — "if error is heavy-tailed, some policies obtain arbitrarily high reward despite achieving no more utility than the base model—a phenomenon we call catastrophic Goodhart" — using KL divergence and heavy-tail asymptotics (Foss–Korshunov–Zachary 2013), NOT any variance-lower-bound estimation lineage. No citation of the three items.
- **Majka & El-Mhamdi 2025 "The Strong, Weak and Benign Goodhart's law" (arXiv 2505.23445)** is pure tail-asymptotics and copula estimation; cites no χ²-divergence/DRO and none of the three items.

### Branch 4 — Corrective-policy Goodhart (Reynaert–Sallee) and second-best

**Verdict: gap REAL in the formal corpus.** Reynaert & Sallee's "Corrective Policy and Goodhart's Law: The Case of Carbon Emissions from Automobiles" (NBER Working Paper No. 22911, 2016; the acknowledgments thank "Meredith Fowlie for introducing us to the term Goodhart's Law in this policy context") is a fully Goodhart-framed economics result on gaming corrective measures. (Note: this is distinct from the related but separate 2021 AEJ: Economic Policy 13(1):372–412 paper "Who Benefits When Firms Game Corrective Policies?") I found no AI-safety / reward-hacking paper that cites either and connects it to reward overoptimization. "Second best" + alignment, and Chetty sufficient-statistics + reward, likewise produced no type-(i) bridge within the reward-hacking corpus.

### Known exceptions re-confirmed
- **CHAI (CIRL 2016, Off-Switch 2017):** type-(ii), control not Goodhart. The Zhuang & Hadfield-Menell 2020 reference cluster confirms the CHAI line cites Holmström-style agency/incomplete-contracting for control/assistive purposes, not as a Goodhart distortion theorem.
- **BBS "Proxy failure" 2023 (Behavioral and Brain Sciences):** cites Baker 2002, Bénabou & Tirole 2016, Kerr 1975 qualitatively — "a 'principal's' incentive schemes are 'gamed' by rational agents (Baker, 2002; Bénabou and Tirole, 2016; … Kerr, 1975)" — no formal theorem. Borderline type-(ii), confirmed.
- **Hendrycks 2021 "Unsolved Problems in ML Safety":** genealogy-tier Ridgway 1956, type-(ii).
- **Wang & Huang 2026:** type-(i) for HM1991 + Baker 1992 only, confirmed.

## Recommendations

- **Treat the χ²-DRO / Chapman–Robbins branch as a genuine, auditable open gap.** This is the cleanest negative: the HCR bound is mathematically identical to the χ²-variance bound used in reward-overoptimization, yet no reward-hacking paper cites it as Goodhart math. A short note formalizing "overoptimization error is lower-bounded by the χ²-divergence between proxy-optimal and true-optimal occupancy measures (HCR bound)" would be a real contribution and is currently uncited. **Benchmark that would change this conclusion:** a single arXiv/OpenReview reward-hacking paper whose reference list contains Chapman–Robbins / Hammersley (or Ben-Tal et al. 2013, or Namkoong–Duchi 2017) AND frames it against proxy-vs-true reward — at which point this branch flips from "fully real" to "partially closed."
- **For the congruity branch, treat the gap as partially closed.** Cite Haupt et al. 2026 and Wang & Huang 2026 as the existing bridges, but note they reach only HM1991/Baker. The Baker cosine-distortion index (1−cosθ), Feltham–Xie congruity, Banker–Datar, and Datar–Kulp–Lambert sensitivity/precision decomposition remain un-bridged as Goodhart geometry — a defensible niche for new work.
- **Do not count strategic-classification / performative-prediction citations as refutations.** Papers like "Manipulation-Proof Machine Learning" cite Holmström 1979 and Hardt et al. 2016 together — that is the strategic-ML / performative-prediction community (Hardt et al. 2016, Perdomo et al. 2020), a different lineage, and does not close the AI-safety corpus gap.
- **If forced to name the single strongest refutation:** Haupt et al. 2026 is the most type-(i)-like bridge findable, because it operationalizes multitask distortion into an AI measurement design and explicitly invokes Goodhart. It does NOT fully close the congruity branch because it stops at HM1991 / Baker 2002 and never reaches the cosine-congruity geometry.

## Caveats
- Several of the strongest bridge papers are very recent (2026) preprints (Haupt et al. arXiv 2605.30916; Wang & Huang arXiv 2603.28063; Liu et al. arXiv 2604.12086) and have not been peer-reviewed; their framing and reference lists could shift in revision.
- My web_search budget was exhausted before I could independently re-fetch the full bibliographies of every 2026 preprint. The subagent verified complete reference lists for Kwa et al. and Majka & El-Mhamdi, but verified only introductions / related-work + visible bibliography for Liu et al. and DARC, so a deeply buried citation in those two cannot be excluded with 100% confidence — though their chosen DRO lineage (Hu & Hong, Lam, Delage & Ye, Goh & Sim, Duchi–Glynn–Namkoong 2021) strongly indicates the three target items are absent.
- The Wang & Huang 2026 details and the DARC citing sentence come from the subagent's retrieval; I was unable to independently re-verify the verbatim Wang & Huang quote against the primary PDF within budget.
- "Type-(i) vs (ii)" classification is a judgment about *purpose*; the borderline cases (Hadfield-Menell & Hadfield; BBS Proxy Failure) could be argued either way, and I have flagged them as borderline rather than forcing a binary. The whole value of this audit rests on that distinction: none of the control/genealogy/DRO-training citations found should be mistaken for a Goodhart bridge.
