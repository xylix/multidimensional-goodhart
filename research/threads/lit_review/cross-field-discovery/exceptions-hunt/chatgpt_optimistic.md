# Cross-Pollination Between AI Reward-Hacking and Older Economics, Accounting, and Statistics

## Bottom line

At the level of **formal mathematics**, the two literatures are **not already well connected**. The strongest bridges I found are recent and clustered in two adjacent subcommunities rather than in the canonical Goodhart / RLHF papers themselves:

First, a **benchmark-design / evaluation** line now explicitly reuses **Holmström–Milgrom multitask agency** and **Baker-style distortion geometry** to model benchmark optimization as a proxy-vs-welfare problem. The clearest examples are **Wang & Huang 2026** (your known floor), **Welfare, Improvability, and Variance** (2026), and **Optimal Aggregation Mechanisms for AI Benchmarking and Platinum Benchmarks** (2026). These papers do not merely name-check the economics; they adapt the model and derive closed-form weighting / distortion results for AI evaluation. citeturn9search8turn10view0turn12view0turn13view0

Second, an **RLHF / robust alignment** line imports **distributionally robust optimization** and **χ²-based regularization** into overoptimization mitigation. The strongest exact bridges here are **Distributionally Robust Reinforcement Learning with Human Feedback** (2025), **Distributionally Robust Token Optimization in RLHF** (2026), and **DRO–REBEL** (2025). These connect reward overoptimization to **Ben-Tal et al. 2013**, **Namkoong–Duchi 2017**, and in DRO–REBEL even to the **Hammersley–Chapman–Robbins lower bound**. But this bridge is mostly through robustness / pessimism / uncertainty quantification, not through explicit “Goodhart’s law” genealogy. citeturn23view0turn22view0turn27view0turn34view0turn35view0

The disappointing part is that I found **very little direct uptake** of the specific older accounting/public-finance items you highlighted—especially **Feltham–Xie congruity**, **Banker–Datar**, **Datar–Kulp–Lambert**, **Chetty sufficiency**, **Reynaert–Sallee**, and **second-best**—inside the **core AI-safety reward-hacking / formal Goodhart / RLHF overoptimization corpus**. Where bridges exist, they are mostly either benchmark-design papers, robustness papers, or adjacent social-measurement / governance work. citeturn21view0turn23view0turn27view0turn32view0turn39view0

## What the strongest bridges actually show

The **best new bridge beyond your floor** is the Stanford benchmark-design line. **Welfare, Improvability, and Variance** does not merely analogize benchmarks to incentives; it explicitly says benchmark gaming is a **Goodhart/Campbell pathology** and then derives a closed-form optimal weighting rule from an adaptation of **Holmström–Milgrom**. Its central claim is that benchmark welfare loss is jointly determined by **welfare alignment**, **improvability**, and **noise**, producing \(v^\*=(M+r\Sigma)^{-1}Mw\). That is a genuine transfer of the multitask-contracting math into AI proxy optimization. citeturn10view0turn12view0turn12view1

The other notable new bridge is **Optimal Aggregation Mechanisms for AI Benchmarking and Platinum Benchmarks**. It makes the Goodhart connection unusually explicit—“when an index becomes the target” is precisely the opening problem statement—and then says the model is a “reinterpretation” of **Holmstrom & Milgrom (1991)** for AI benchmark design. The paper is narrower than Wang & Huang 2026, but it is still a real formal bridge from classical multitask distortion theory to AI evaluation incentives. citeturn13view0

On the **RLHF** side, the best bridge is not to contract theory but to **DRO / χ² geometry**. **Distributionally Robust Reinforcement Learning with Human Feedback** explicitly starts from the empirical observation that RLHF suffers from “overoptimization” where proxy reward rises while true reward falls, and then imports **Ben-Tal et al. 2013** as the formal language for robustifying both reward estimation and policy optimization. **Distributionally Robust Token Optimization in RLHF** pushes that further by explicitly citing **Namkoong–Duchi 2017** as the source of the variance-sensitive / entropic-risk interpretation for divergence-based DRO, and it places **χ²-Preference Optimization** in the same overoptimization-mitigation lineage. **DRO–REBEL** goes furthest mathematically: it ties RLHF overoptimization to **χ² ambiguity sets**, cites **Namkoong–Duchi 2017**, and even uses **Hammersley–Chapman–Robbins** in the technical machinery. citeturn23view0turn22view0turn27view0turn34view0turn35view0

The one place I found your requested **Feltham–Xie congruity** bridge was **The Content Moderator’s Dilemma**. That paper is not in the core “AI alignment theory” canon, but it is close enough to be interesting: it explicitly uses **Holmström–Milgrom** plus **Feltham–Xie** to argue that moderation policies over-invest in measurable toxicity removal at the expense of harder-to-measure discourse quality. That is a genuinely nontrivial accounting-theory → AI-governance bridge. citeturn21view0

## How connected the literatures actually are

My current read is:

The literatures are **moderately connected at the level of vocabulary and analogy**, but only **thinly connected at the level of explicit shared mathematics**. The mathematics is there to be reused, and a few recent papers do reuse it, but the reuse is **far from pervasive** in the core AI-safety / reward-hacking canon. The strongest connections I found are not mostly in **Manheim–Garrabrant / Skalse / Gao–Schulman–Hilton / Rafailov / Laidlaw** themselves; they are mostly in **adjacent benchmark-design papers** and **RLHF robustness papers**. citeturn10view0turn13view0turn23view0turn27view0turn34view0

So the short answer is: **the bridge exists, but it is not yet dense**. The contract-theory side is newly getting real uptake in AI benchmarking. The DRO / robust-statistics side is more active in RLHF. But I did **not** find evidence that the mainstream Goodhart / reward-hacking literature has already deeply integrated **Feltham–Xie congruity**, **Banker–Datar aggregation**, **Chetty sufficiency**, **Reynaert–Sallee**, or **second-best** as common formal reference points. citeturn21view0turn23view0turn27view0turn32view0turn39view0

## Open questions and limitations

I would treat three areas as still open rather than closed negatives.

First, **Banker–Datar / Datar–Kulp–Lambert / Şabac–Yoo** may still be hiding in the emerging **AI benchmarking / eval aggregation** literature; I found the principal-agent benchmark papers, but not those more specific accounting aggregation references. citeturn10view0turn13view0

Second, **Chetty sufficiency**, **Feldstein**, and **Reynaert–Sallee** may be more likely to show up in **AI policy / public economics / AI governance** papers than in the ML-alignment corpus proper. In this session I did not find a clean core-ML hit. citeturn41search6turn40search3

Third, the **strategic-classification / performative-prediction** lineage is indeed a distinct community. Some of the benchmark and aggregation papers I found are closer to that world than to classic alignment theory, which is itself evidence for your underlying hypothesis: the math has been rediscovered or imported, but often through **evaluation**, **robustness**, or **strategic behavior** subfields rather than through canonical AI-safety papers. citeturn15view3turn10view0turn13view0
