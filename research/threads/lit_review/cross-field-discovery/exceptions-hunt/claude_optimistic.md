# Cross-Pollination Between AI-Safety Goodhart/Reward-Hacking Research and the Classical Economics/Statistics Prior Art: A Connection Map

## TL;DR
- The two literatures are connected at the level of the **eponym and the qualitative idea**, but **thinly connected at the level of the shared mathematics**: only a handful of AI-safety papers cite the classical contract-theory or distributionally-robust-optimization (DRO) results, and most that reinvent the same math (cosine-distortion geometry, χ²-divergence robustness) do so **without citing the prior art**.
- I found **three formal bridges beyond the four "known" ones**: (1) the χ²-DRO robust-RLHF line — **DRO-REBEL** (arXiv:2509.19104) and **Robust DPO** (arXiv:2502.01930) — which explicitly cite **Namkoong–Duchi 2017** (Hongseok Namkoong & John C. Duchi, "Variance-based Regularization with Convex Objectives," NeurIPS 30, 2017, which itself "builds off of techniques for distributionally robust optimization") and tie χ²-divergence DRO to reward overoptimization; (2) **"Robust Optimization for Mitigating Reward Hacking with Correlated Proxies"** (arXiv:2604.12086), which cites the DRO foundations **Lam 2016, Hu–Hong, Delage–Ye, Goh–Sim** and ties them to a formal reward-hacking result; and (3) the contract-theory genealogy of **Hadfield-Menell et al. "Incomplete Contracting and AI Alignment"** (arXiv:1804.04268), which re-states Holmström–Milgrom 1991 multitask distortion but frames it as incomplete-contracting, not Goodhart.
- Several specific prior-art items the user named have **NO traceable AI-safety citation at all** (auditable negatives): **Feltham–Xie 1994 congruity, Banker–Datar 1989, Chetty 2009 sufficient statistics, Hammersley–Chapman–Robbins, Cramér–Rao, Reynaert–Sallee 2016, Lipsey–Lancaster 1956**. These are genuine gaps where the same math exists on both sides but no one has drawn the line.

## Key Findings

1. **The strongest single formal bridge remains Wang & Huang 2026** (arXiv:2603.28063, "Reward Hacking as Equilibrium under Finite Evaluation"). It instantiates the **Holmström–Milgrom 1991** multitask principal-agent model (Bengt Holmström & Paul Milgrom, "Multitask Principal–Agent Analyses: Incentive Contracts, Asset Ownership, and Job Design," *Journal of Law, Economics, & Organization*, Vol. 7, Special Issue, 1991, pp. 24–52) and **Baker 1992** distortion result inside AI alignment and derives a computable "distortion index." Important correction to the user's prior: the distortion index is a **weight ratio** D_i ≡ w̃_i/w_i, **not** the Baker-2002 cosine/angle (1−cosθ) geometry, and the paper cites **Baker 1992, not Baker 2002**; it does **not** cite Feltham–Xie, Banker–Datar, Holmström 1979, or Bénabou–Tirole.

2. **The χ²-DRO reward-hacking cluster is where the deepest math overlap lives — and the citation behavior is split.** Papers in this cluster all rediscover that χ²-divergence (rather than KL) is the right ambiguity geometry for preventing proxy overoptimization:
   - **Laidlaw, Singhal & Dragan 2024 "Correlated Proxies"** (arXiv:2403.03185, submitted 5 Mar 2024, v4 revised 13 Mar 2025, ICLR 2025) gives a formal proxy-vs-true definition of reward hacking and proves χ²-occupancy-measure regularization mitigates it — but **does not cite** Ben-Tal 2013, Namkoong–Duchi 2017, Lam 2016, Hu–Hong, or Delage–Ye. The math is reinvented.
   - **Huang et al. 2025 χPO** (arXiv:2407.13399) implements χ²-divergence pessimism to provably alleviate overoptimization, but grounds χ² via **Tsybakov 2008** and an f-divergence reparameterization (Wang et al. 2023a), **not** the DRO lineage.
   - In contrast, **DRO-REBEL** (arXiv:2509.19104) and **Robust DPO** (arXiv:2502.01930) **do** cite **Namkoong–Duchi 2017** (variance regularization = χ²-DRO) as the foundation, and the follow-up **"Robust Optimization for Mitigating Reward Hacking with Correlated Proxies"** (arXiv:2604.12086) cites **Lam 2016, Hu–Hong, Delage–Ye, Goh–Sim**. These are formal estimation/DRO → reward-overoptimization bridges that go beyond the four known ones.

3. **Contract theory enters AI safety mainly through the CHAI/Berkeley control lineage, not the Goodhart lineage.** Hadfield-Menell et al. "Incomplete Contracting and AI Alignment" (arXiv:1804.04268) re-states Holmström–Milgrom 1991 and Baker et al. 1994 (the teaching-to-the-test and auto-repair examples) and maps reward misspecification to multitasking — a deep restatement, but framed as incomplete contracting / control, not as a formal Goodhart theorem. CIRL (2016) and the Off-Switch Game (2017) cite the same body for agency/control.

4. **The management/social-measurement genealogy (Ridgway 1956, Kerr 1975, Campbell 1979, Strathern 1997) is cited widely but only at the eponym/folklore tier.** Hendrycks et al. "Unsolved Problems in ML Safety" (2021) and the wellbeing-estimation paper cite **Ridgway 1956** (V. F. Ridgway, "Dysfunctional Consequences of Performance Measurements," *Administrative Science Quarterly*, Vol. 1, No. 2, Sep. 1956, pp. 240–247 — "indiscriminate use and undue confidence and reliance in them result from insufficient knowledge of the full effects and consequences … The cure is sometimes worse than the disease") for "imperfect proxy metrics"; Ashton 2021 "Causal Campbell–Goodhart's Law and RL" cites Campbell 1979. None re-derive a theorem from these.

5. **The "Proxy Failure" BBS target article** (Yohan J. John, Leigh Caldwell, Dakota E. McCoy & Oliver Braganza, "Dead rats, dopamine, performance metrics, and peacock tails: Proxy failure is an inherent risk in goal-oriented systems," *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, Vol. 47, 2023, pp. 1–68, published 26 June 2023) is the most concentrated point of contact with the contract-theory prior art (Holmström 1979/2017, Baker 2002, Bénabou–Tirole 2016, Kerr 1975), but qualitatively — casting reward-model design as "optimal incentive contract" design with "distortion" — with **no formal theorem** and outside the core AI-safety reward-hacking corpus.

6. **Strategic-classification / performative-prediction is a distinct community** (correctly flagged in the task). It does touch contract theory: "Power and Limitations of Aggregation in Compound AI Systems" (arXiv:2602.21556) cites Holmström–Milgrom 1991 alongside strategic-classification work — specifically Safwan Hossain, Evi Micha, Yiling Chen & Ariel Procaccia, "Strategic Classification with Externalities" (arXiv:2410.08032, 2024) and Meena Jagadeesan, Michael Jordan, Jacob Steinhardt & Nika Haghtalab, "Improved Bayes risk can yield reduced social welfare under competition." These should not be conflated with the reward-hacking corpus.

### Notable individual citing sentences (verbatim)
- Wang & Huang 2026: "Our framework instantiates the multi-task principal-agent model of Holmström and Milgrom (1991) … to derive a computable *distortion index* that predicts both the direction and severity of hacking on each quality dimension prior to deployment." And: "Baker (1992) showed that when performance measures imperfectly correlate with true objectives, incentive contracts induce systematic distortion." Distortion index definition (Definition 3): D_i ≡ w̃_i/w_i, equal to λ·(r_i/w_i) + (1−λ) for contractible dimensions and (1−λ) for non-contractible dimensions — a weight ratio, not a cosine.
- Laidlaw et al. 2024: "While the current practice in RLHF applies a KL penalty between action distributions for this purpose, our theory suggests regularizing the χ² divergence between the policies' occupancy measures can be more effective."
- DRO-REBEL: "The DRO framework has been applied to various areas ranging from supervised learning (Namkoong and Duchi, 2017b; …)."
- Hadfield-Menell et al. 2018: "Holmstrom & Milgrom (1991) and Baker et al. (1994) show that the optimal incentive contract for a task that can be measured should take into account the impact of those rewards on effort put towards tasks that cannot be measured."

## Recommendations

**For a researcher trying to build or document the bridge:**

1. **Treat the χ²-DRO reward-hacking cluster as the live frontier.** The single highest-value, under-cited connection is between **Namkoong–Duchi 2017 / Ben-Tal 2013 / Lam 2016 (χ²-DRO variance regularization)** and **Laidlaw 2024 / χPO 2025**. Laidlaw and χPO independently rediscovered that χ² is the correct ambiguity geometry for proxy overoptimization but **did not cite** the DRO prior art; DRO-REBEL and the 2604.12086 follow-up later supplied the missing citations. A short note formally identifying χ²-occupancy regularization with classical χ²-DRO ambiguity sets would close a real gap. **Benchmark that would change this recommendation:** if a survey or paper already explicitly states "Laidlaw's χ² regularization is an instance of Namkoong–Duchi χ²-DRO," the gap is closed — I found no such statement.

2. **The Baker-2002 cosine-distortion geometry is the most promising un-built formal bridge.** Karwowski et al. 2023 give a *geometric* (angle-between-proxy-and-true) explanation of Goodhart in MDPs; Baker 2002 defines distortion as 1−cosθ between performance-measure and true-value gradients. **No one has connected these two cosine geometries.** This is a clean, publishable derivation. Wang & Huang 2026 used Baker *1992* and a weight-ratio index, leaving the cosine version open.

3. **Feltham–Xie congruity and Banker–Datar aggregation are entirely unexploited.** The Feltham–Xie proportionality (congruity) condition is mathematically the "unhackability" condition of Skalse et al. 2022 in different language; the Banker–Datar optimal-weight result is the reward-aggregation problem of multi-objective RLHF / Moskovitz constrained RLHF. Citing them would be novel — **currently zero AI-safety citations exist.**

4. **Do not over-claim connectedness.** Anyone asserting these literatures are "already well connected at the math level" is **overstating the evidence.** The connection is dense at the eponym/folklore tier (Goodhart, Campbell, Ridgway, Kerr) and at the qualitative contract-theory framing (Proxy Failure, incomplete-contracting), but the *formal* theorem-to-theorem bridges number roughly five papers, three of which appeared in 2025–2026.

## Caveats
- **Citation-graph completeness:** Reverse-citation coverage from public APIs (Semantic Scholar, OpenCitations, dblp) is incomplete, especially for 2025–2026 arXiv preprints and for appendix-only bibliographies. My negatives are "no citation found via the searches listed," not proof of absence; appendix reference lists I could not machine-read may contain a stray citation.
- **A direct conflict I could not fully resolve:** an automated reference-API check reported that Laidlaw et al. 2024 does *not* cite Lam/Hu–Hong/Delage–Ye, while the closely-titled 2026 follow-up "Robust Optimization for Mitigating Reward Hacking with Correlated Proxies" (arXiv:2604.12086) clearly *does* (refs [28]–[31]). I attribute the DRO citations to the **follow-up paper**, not Laidlaw's original, but readers should verify the original's appendix directly.
- **Several arXiv IDs in the 2602–2604 range are dated 2026** (e.g., 2603.28063, 2604.12086, 2604.13602) and are recent preprints that may not be peer-reviewed; treat their claims as preprint-stage. Wang & Huang 2026's "first economic formalization of the treacherous turn" is a self-described contribution including **conjectures with only partial formal analysis** (the Goodhart→Campbell capability-threshold claims).
- **The "distortion index" in Wang & Huang is a weight ratio, not Baker's cosine** — the user's prior framing that it "derives a distortion index [from] Baker 1992" is correct in spirit but the geometry differs from the Baker-2002 1−cosθ formulation, which remains uncited in the AI-safety corpus.
