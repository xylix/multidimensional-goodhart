# Referee Report: Precedents for a "New" Multidimensional Goodhart's Law Model

## TL;DR
- Five of the six pieces have established, named precedents in personnel economics, managerial accounting, and public finance; only the explicit *measurement-gaming reading* of certain optimization results is a genuine repackaging contribution, not the underlying mathematics.
- The decisive blow: the multitask agency literature (Holmström-Milgrom 1991; Feltham-Xie 1994; Baker 1992/2002; Datar-Kulp-Lambert 2001) already unifies Pieces 1, 2, 3, and 4 inside a single linear-agency framework — so the colleague's "synthesis" of those four is largely a relabeling of one literature.
- The strongest claim to novelty is integrative/cross-disciplinary framing (uniting the accounting congruity object, the public-finance sufficient-statistic object, and the Goodhart/gaming reading under one notation), not any individual component.

## Key Findings
- **Piece 1 (equimarginal gaming load):** NOT NOVEL. It is the standard first-order condition of the linear multitask model (Baker 1992 eq. 3; Holmström-Milgrom 1991 vector-effort FOC). Only the optimization is there in the source; the "gaming" reading is already present in Baker's patent example.
- **Piece 2 (distortion/congruity, h_j = c·w_j):** NOT NOVEL. This IS the Feltham-Xie (1994) congruity condition and Baker's (2000/2002) cosine/angle distortion measure — proportionality of the sensitivity vector to the value vector. The colleague's condition is the same object up to relabeling.
- **Piece 3 (set non-monotonicity):** REPACKAGING. Datar-Kulp-Lambert (2001) prove adding/increasing a measure can help or hurt; Lipsey-Lancaster (1956) is the general second-best root. Neither states "subset-invariance" as a named property — it's an unstated corollary.
- **Piece 4 (lossless scalarization / sufficiency):** NOT NOVEL. Banker-Datar (1989) give the exact condition under which a linear aggregate is a sufficient statistic (weights ∝ sensitivity × precision); Holmström (1979) informativeness principle and Chetty (2009) are the agency and public-finance analogues.
- **Piece 5 (corrective-policy Goodhart):** NOT NOVEL. Reynaert-Sallee (2016/2021) is the exact "home"; Frankel-Kartik (2019) supplies the stakes→info-loss mechanism; second-best logic is Lipsey-Lancaster.
- **Piece 6 (selection pressure → harm):** NOT NOVEL. Campbell's Law (1979) states it verbally; Frankel-Kartik (2019) and Benabou-Tirole (2016) formalize stakes/competition → distortion.

## Details

### Piece 1 — Least-cost / equimarginal gaming load (a_j ∝ κ_j·w_j)
**Best precedent: Baker (1992), "Incentive Contracts and Performance Measurement," JPE 100(3):598-614.** Baker's agent first-order condition (eq. 3) is bP_e(e*,ε) = C′(e*): marginal incentive (piece-rate × marginal product of effort on the measure) equals marginal cost. Baker explicitly states (pp. 602-603) that effort "can be thought of as a vector of tasks" and that the agent "chooses not simply how hard to work but also how to allocate her effort across tasks," and his Section IV patent example is an explicit gaming reading ("the scientist is able to game the number of patents... work too hard on the easy ones"). With a convex (quadratic) cost, solving the vector FOC gives effort on each channel proportional to the incentive intensity on that channel scaled by its marginal-cost coefficient — exactly the κ_j·w_j equimarginal allocation.

**Foundational multidimensional version: Holmström-Milgrom (1991), JLEO 7:24-52.** The agent "chooses a vector t = (t₁,…,tₘ) at a personal (strictly convex) cost C(t₁+…+tₘ)." The vector FOC ∇C(t) = (incentive intensities) yields, for a separable quadratic cost, effort on task j proportional to the incentive weight divided by its marginal cost.

**Exact divergence:** These papers state the equimarginal allocation as the agent's *optimal effort response*, not as a *gaming-load* distribution over harmful channels. The structure is identical; what the colleague adds is the interpretive label "gaming load" and (apparently) a separate cost-weight κ_j held distinct from the incentive weight. In Holmström-Milgrom and Baker the cost structure and the incentive weights are precisely the two ingredients, so the κ_j·w_j product is their FOC. **Verdict: only-the-optimization-is-there in the source (case iii); the gaming reading is Baker's own (case i for that example). Classify NOT NOVEL.** The closest "least-cost margin" verbal statement outside agency theory is in the elasticity-of-taxable-income literature (Saez-Slemrod-Giertz 2012): tax-induced behavior occurs "until, at the margin, the private cost equals the tax saving," so avoidance flows to the cheapest margin first.

### Piece 2 — Distortion / congruity (hidden harm = weighted average of h_j/w_j; zero distortion iff h_j = c·w_j)
**Best precedent: Feltham-Xie (1994), "Performance Measure Congruity and Diversity in Multi-Task Principal/Agent Relations," The Accounting Review 69(3):429-453.** In the linear multitask model the principal's value is V(a) = d′a (d = vector of marginal products / value weights) and the performance measure is P_j = y_j′a + ε_j (y_j = sensitivity vector). The measure is *congruent* iff its sensitivity vector is proportional to the value vector — i.e., there exists λ ≠ 0 with y = λd. This is verified verbatim in the Budde (2007, JAR) treatment of the same model: "there exists no constant λ ≠ 0 satisfying μ = λω, thereby implying φ = 0. A more congruent performance measure thereby implies a smaller angle φ." (I confirmed the model setup — V=d′a, P_j=y_j′a+ε_j — verbatim from the Budde PDF.)

**Baker (2000/2002):** distortion = 1 − cos(θ), the angle between the performance-measure gradient and the firm-value gradient. Datar-Kulp-Lambert (2001) and Budde generalize the cosine to multiple measures (the squared cosine is the Rayleigh quotient / "congruity index φ(d,Y)").

**Is h_j = c·w_j VERBATIM theirs?** Essentially yes, up to which vector is named "value" and which is "harm." The colleague writes zero distortion iff the harm vector is proportional to the weight vector (h_j = c·w_j). Feltham-Xie write zero incongruity iff the sensitivity vector is proportional to the value vector (y = λd). These are the **same proportionality-of-two-vectors condition**; the colleague's "harm-per-unit-weight exchange rate h_j/w_j" is the component-wise ratio that is constant exactly when the vectors are collinear. **Statement confirmed; the model setup and proportionality/cosine condition verified.**

**Exact divergence:** (a) Feltham-Xie/Baker define congruity over the **sensitivity-vs-value** pair (how the measure responds to actions vs. how value responds), while the colleague frames it as **harm-vs-weight**. If "harm" is defined as the value lost when effort is misallocated, these coincide; if "harm" is an independent externality vector, the mapping is *analogous, not identical*. (b) The 1−cos(θ) "distortion-as-angle" is a *scalar magnitude* of incongruity, whereas the colleague's "weighted average of exchange rates h_j/w_j" is a *different scalarization* of the same misalignment; they agree on the zero-distortion knife-edge (collinearity) but **diverge off that knife-edge** — the angle metric is invariant to rescaling each vector, the weighted-average-of-ratios metric is not. **Verdict: NOT NOVEL for the zero-distortion condition (it is Feltham-Xie verbatim in substance); the specific off-axis scalarization may differ from Baker's cosine but is one of several known incongruity indices.**

### Piece 3 — Set non-monotonicity (adding a channel can raise or lower harm; count is not the governing variable)
**Best precedent: Datar-Kulp-Lambert (2001), "Balancing Performance Measures," JAR 39(1):75-92.** Their stated result: "an increase in the sensitivity of a performance measure to an agent's action does not necessarily increase the weight" placed on it, and adding measures trades off congruity against risk. They show the optimal weight on a measure can be zero or negative and that adding a measure can raise or lower total surplus.

**General root: Lipsey-Lancaster (1956), "The General Theory of Second Best," REStud 24(1):11-32.** "If there is introduced into a general equilibrium system a constraint which prevents the attainment of one of the Paretian conditions, the other Paretian conditions, although still attainable, are, in general, no longer desirable." This is the canonical statement that satisfying *more* optimality conditions (or here, adding more correctly-measured channels) need not improve welfare — the count of satisfied conditions is not monotone in welfare.

**Is SET-MONOTONICITY actually STATED, or only IMPLIED?** In Datar-Kulp-Lambert the non-monotonic *value of an individual measure* is stated outright (case i); the *subset-invariance / non-monotonicity in the measured-channel-set* as a named, general property is **not stated — it is a one-line corollary (case ii)**. Lipsey-Lancaster state the welfare non-monotonicity in the *number of satisfied conditions* as their central theorem (case i), but in the public-finance, not the measurement-gaming, register (case iii for the gaming reading). **Exact divergence:** neither source isolates "the count of channels is not the governing variable" as a theorem about the *measured set*; the colleague's contribution here is to *name* the property. **Verdict: REPACKAGING — an unstated corollary of Datar-Kulp-Lambert plus the second-best theorem.**

### Piece 4 — Sufficiency / lossless scalarization (Σ w_j P_j is a sufficient statistic for true P only under a condition)
**Best precedent: Banker-Datar (1989), "Sensitivity, Precision, and Linear Aggregation of Signals for Performance Evaluation," JAR 27(1):21-39.** They "identify necessary and sufficient conditions on the joint density function of the signals under which linear aggregation... is optimal," and prove (confirmed verbatim from the original) that "the relative weight on each signal in the optimal performance evaluation measure for incentive purposes is directly proportional to the product of the sensitivity and [precision]." This is precisely the lossless-scalarization condition: a scalar weighted sum is a sufficient statistic for the multidimensional signal set exactly when the weights are sensitivity×precision and the signal density is in the right (exponential-type) class. Amershi-Banker-Datar (1990, "Economic Sufficiency and Statistical Sufficiency in Accounting Aggregation," Accounting Review) is the direct follow-up tying statistical sufficiency to economic sufficiency.

**Agency root: Holmström (1979), "Moral Hazard and Observability," Bell Journal 10:74-91.** The informativeness principle: a signal has value iff output is not a sufficient statistic for (output, signal) with respect to effort. Sufficiency is exactly the condition under which the extra signal is *not* needed — the dual of lossless aggregation.

**Public-finance analogue: Chetty (2009), "Is the Taxable Income Elasticity Sufficient to Calculate Deadweight Loss?", AEJ:Policy 1(2):31-52.** The taxable-income elasticity is a sufficient statistic for deadweight loss *only* when the marginal social cost of evasion/avoidance equals the tax rate (Feldstein's assumption); when avoidance involves transfers or resource costs, "excess burden depends on a weighted average of the taxable income and total earned income elasticities, with the weight determined by the resource cost of sheltering income."

**Do any state the lossless-scalarization condition?** Yes — Banker-Datar (1989) state it *for the contracting problem* (case i, with exact condition). Chetty (2009) states the analogous sufficiency knife-edge for welfare (case i, public-finance register). **Exact divergence:** Banker-Datar's condition is about *statistical* sufficiency of a linear aggregate of *signals* for *inferring effort*, not about a weighted sum being a sufficient statistic for an underlying *multidimensional true-performance vector*. The colleague's object (scalar Σw_jP_j sufficient for vector P) is closest to Banker-Datar but is technically the reverse direction (aggregating measures vs. aggregating signals about a scalar action). Chetty's is about a scalar elasticity, not a vector collapse. **Verdict: NOT NOVEL — Banker-Datar is the exact home for the accounting version, Chetty for the welfare version. The colleague's framing as "lossless scalarization of a performance vector" is a mild generalization (case ii relative to Banker-Datar).**

### Piece 5 — Second-best / corrective-policy Goodhart
**Best precedent: Reynaert-Sallee (2016 NBER WP22911 / 2021 AEJ:Policy "Who Benefits When Firms Game Corrective Policies?", 13(1):372-412, DOI 10.1257/pol.20190019).** Exact framing: "Firms sometimes comply with externality-correcting policies by gaming the measure that determines policy... policy alters the accuracy of the measure. This is Goodhart's Law." The published paper's keyword list explicitly pairs "Goodhart's Law" with "Gaming, corrective taxation, Environmental Regulation, fuel economy" — it is the *exact* corrective-policy-Goodhart object. **Key divergence to flag:** their headline welfare result is that gaming can *benefit* buyers. Verbatim from the AEJ:Policy abstract: "Over a wide range of parameters, we find that pass-through substantially outweighs information distortions; on net, buyers benefit from gaming, even when they are fooled by it." If the colleague's model treats gaming as unambiguously harmful, this is a substantive divergence from the best Piece-5 precedent, not a relabeling.

**Mechanism precedent: Frankel-Kartik (2019), "Muddled Information," JPE 127(4):1739-1776.** "As incentives to take higher actions increase — due to higher stakes or more manipulable signaling technology — more information is revealed about gaming ability, and less about natural actions." This supplies the "proxy degrades when used for policy" microfoundation.

**General root: Lipsey-Lancaster (1956).** The second-best logic that a corrective instrument interacting with an existing distortion (the proxy) need not improve welfare. **Verdict: NOT NOVEL — Reynaert-Sallee is the exact home, with Frankel-Kartik and Lipsey-Lancaster as the mechanism and the general principle.**

### Piece 6 — Selection-pressure strength → harm magnitude
**Best precedent: Campbell's Law — Campbell, D.T. (1979), "Assessing the Impact of Planned Social Change," Evaluation and Program Planning 2(1):67-90 (DOI 10.1016/0149-7189(79)90048-X).** "The more any quantitative social indicator is used for social decision-making, the more subject it will be to corruption pressures and the more apt it will be to distort and corrupt the social processes it is intended to monitor." This is the verbal statement of "stronger optimization pressure → more corruption" (case i, informal).

**Formalization: Frankel-Kartik (2019)** — higher stakes monotonically reduce the signal's informativeness about the natural action. **Benabou-Tirole (2016), "Bonus Culture," JPE 124(2):305-370** — verbatim from the abstract: "Competition for talent leads to an escalation of performance pay, shifting effort away from long-term investments, risk management, and cooperation. Efficiency losses can exceed those from a single principal... Aggregate welfare is thus hill-shaped, while inequality tends to rise monotonically." Stronger competitive (selection) pressure increases multitask distortion. **Verdict: NOT NOVEL — Campbell's Law states it; Frankel-Kartik and Benabou-Tirole formalize it.** (Note: I could not independently verify the specific "Braganza 2022" citation's content from the sources gathered; treat that attribution as unconfirmed.)

### The decisive question — unifying sources
**The multitask linear-agency literature already unifies Pieces 1, 2, 3, and 4.** Within the single Feltham-Xie (1994) / Holmström-Milgrom (1991) / Baker (2000/2002) / Datar-Kulp-Lambert (2001) framework, all of the following appear together: the equimarginal effort FOC (Piece 1), the congruity/distortion proportionality condition (Piece 2), the non-monotone value of adding/weighting a measure (Piece 3), and — via Banker-Datar (1989), part of the same managerial-accounting school — the sufficient-aggregation condition (Piece 4). **This is the single most damaging finding for a "synthesis" claim: four of six pieces are already co-located in one literature.**

**Surveys that tie multiple pieces together:**
- **Prendergast (1999), "The Provision of Incentives in Firms," JEL 37(1):7-63** explicitly ties multitasking/distortion (Pieces 1-2) to the objective-measure problem and "currying favor," and surveys the risk-distortion tradeoff. It does NOT formalize sufficiency or set non-monotonicity.
- **Holmström Nobel lecture (2017), "Pay for Performance and Beyond"** ties the informativeness principle (Piece 4) directly to the emergence of multitasking (Pieces 1-2): "The Informativeness Principle revealed the basic logic of the model... Our willingness to listen to the basic agency model introduced us... into the world of multitasking." This unifies Pieces 4 and 1-2 in one narrative.
- **Holmström-Milgrom (1991)** itself unifies Pieces 1-3 (effort allocation, distorted incentives from imperfect measures, the case for low-powered incentives / not adding strong measures).
- **Ridgway (1956), "Dysfunctional Consequences of Performance Measurements," ASQ 1(2):240-247** is the pre-Goodhart (by 19 years) verbal source that already discusses single, multiple, AND composite (weighted-sum) measures and notes that a composite measure "tends to reduce the motivation to reduce effort in one area to improve another" — an informal precursor to Pieces 2-3 about weighted-sum measures. It is verbal, not formal.

### Older roots (earliest substantiated precedent per piece)
- **Pieces 2-3 (composite/weighted-sum measures, dysfunction):** Ridgway (1956) explicitly analyzes composite weighted measures; Blau (1955, *The Dynamics of Bureaucracy*, pp. 40-44) documents quota-driven gaming in a state employment agency (ordering cases by quota not priority; double-counting placements). These predate Goodhart (1975) and Campbell (1979).
- **Piece 4 (sufficiency/aggregation of signals):** Marschak-Radner (*Economic Theory of Teams*, 1972) and Blackwell's ordering are the team-theory roots for sufficient aggregation of information; Holmström (1979) is the agency formalization. (I was unable to retrieve a verbatim Marschak-Radner statement in this pass — statement plausible from standard citations but exact condition not verified here.)
- **Piece 5 (corrective taxation / equimarginal):** Pigou (corrective taxation) and Ramsey (1927) are the welfare-economics roots for the corrective-policy and equimarginal-sacrifice logic.
- **Piece 6:** Campbell (1979); arguably the behavioral-theory-of-the-firm tradition (Cyert-March 1963; March-Simon 1958) on sub-goal pursuit, and Simon's bounded rationality / sub-goal identification, are older organizational roots.
- **Kerr (1975), "On the Folly of Rewarding A While Hoping for B," AMJ 18(4):769-783** is the classic verbal statement spanning Pieces 2 and 6.

### Recent formal Goodhart papers (additional precedents surfaced)
- **Manheim-Garrabrant (2018), "Categorizing Variants of Goodhart's Law"** (arXiv 1803.04585), building on Scott Garrabrant's "Goodhart Taxonomy" (MIRI, Mar 27 2018): "there are (at least) four different mechanisms through which proxy measures break when you optimize for them: Regressional, Extremal, Causal, and Adversarial." A multidimensional model should be positioned against this taxonomy; the colleague's "adding a channel can raise or lower harm" overlaps with Extremal/Causal Goodhart.
- **Courty-Marschke (2004), "An Empirical Investigation of Gaming Responses to Explicit Performance Incentives," JLabEcon 22(1):23-56** — empirical gaming in JTPA; the canonical applied gaming reference.
- **Lazear (2006), "Speeding, Terrorism, and Teaching to the Test," QJE 121(3):1029-1061** — multitask/teaching-to-the-test angle with the announced-vs-random monitoring tradeoff.

## Recommendations
1. **Concede Pieces 1, 2, 4, 5, 6 as not novel in their mathematical content.** Require the colleague to cite Baker (1992), Holmström-Milgrom (1991), Feltham-Xie (1994), Banker-Datar (1989), Holmström (1979), Chetty (2009), Reynaert-Sallee (2016/2021), Frankel-Kartik (2019), Benabou-Tirole (2016), and Campbell (1979) as direct precedents, and to state explicitly for each whether the contribution is (i) restatement, (ii) corollary, or (iii) a new gaming reading of an existing optimization.
2. **Demand precise positioning of Piece 2.** Require the colleague to show whether their h_j/w_j scalarization coincides with, or differs off-axis from, Baker's 1−cos(θ) and the Datar-Kulp-Lambert/Budde Rayleigh-quotient congruity index. If it coincides, drop the novelty claim; if it differs, that off-axis metric is the only defensible new object in Piece 2.
3. **Treat Piece 3 as a naming contribution only.** Ask whether "set non-monotonicity / subset-invariance" is proved as a general theorem about the measured set (beyond the per-measure non-monotonicity in Datar-Kulp-Lambert and the second-best theorem). If not, it is a corollary.
4. **Narrow the synthesis claim.** Because the multitask literature already co-locates Pieces 1-4, the colleague's genuine contribution can only be the *cross-register unification* — porting the accounting congruity object and the public-finance sufficient-statistic object into a single explicit Goodhart/gaming notation, and positioning against the Manheim-Garrabrant taxonomy. Require the author to state this narrowed claim explicitly.
5. **Benchmark for accepting any novelty:** a piece survives as novel only if (a) no listed precedent states the condition, (b) it is not a one-line corollary of a listed result, and (c) the gaming reading is not already present (as it is in Baker's patent example and Reynaert-Sallee). On current evidence, no single piece clears all three bars; the integrative framing is the only defensible contribution.

## Final Headline
- **Clearly NOT-NOVEL (exact precedent exists, condition stated):** Piece 1 (Baker 1992 / Holmström-Milgrom 1991 FOC), Piece 2 (Feltham-Xie 1994 congruity = vector proportionality; Baker 2000/2002 1−cosθ), Piece 4 (Banker-Datar 1989 lossless linear aggregation; Chetty 2009 sufficiency knife-edge), Piece 5 (Reynaert-Sallee 2016/2021, with "Goodhart's Law" in the title and keywords), Piece 6 (Campbell 1979 stated; Benabou-Tirole 2016 / Frankel-Kartik 2019 formalize).
- **REPACKAGING (unstated one-line corollary):** Piece 3 (set non-monotonicity / subset-invariance) — implied by Datar-Kulp-Lambert 2001 and Lipsey-Lancaster 1956 but named, not derived anew.
- **Plausibly novel after exhausting searches:** No individual piece survives. The only defensible novelty is the *cross-disciplinary unification* (accounting congruity + public-finance sufficiency + Goodhart taxonomy under one notation) — and even that is weakened because the multitask literature already unifies four of six.
- **Does a single source unify several pieces?** Yes. The multitask linear-agency literature (Holmström-Milgrom 1991 + Feltham-Xie 1994 + Baker + Datar-Kulp-Lambert 2001 + Banker-Datar 1989) unifies Pieces 1-4. Holmström's 2017 Nobel lecture narratively links Pieces 1-2 with 4. Ridgway (1956) is the pre-Goodhart verbal unifier of the composite-measure intuitions (Pieces 2-3). This materially shrinks the "synthesis" contribution.
- **New precedents/unifiers surfaced beyond the briefed lists:** Budde (2007, JAR) — explicit verbatim statement of the congruity/cosine condition and the Rayleigh-quotient generalization; Amershi-Banker-Datar (1990) on economic vs. statistical sufficiency (directly Piece 4); Saez-Slemrod-Giertz (2012) for the "least-cost margin" verbal statement (Piece 1); the Reynaert-Sallee welfare-positive gaming result as a *divergence* (Piece 5).
