# Prior-Art / Priority-of-Discovery Investigation: "Multidimensional Goodhart"

## TL;DR
- **The unifying claim is substantially deflated, not destroyed.** At least three single sources already connect two or more of the six component pieces inside one framework: the multitask LEN lineage (Holmström-Milgrom 1991; Baker 2002; Feltham-Xie 1994; Datar-Kulp-Lambert 2001) connects pieces 1, 2, 4, and 6; Reynaert-Sallee 2016 connects pieces 1, 5, and 6 for a regulator gaming a proxy; and John-Caldwell-McCoy-Braganza 2023 (the "proxy failure" BBS article) connects pieces 3, 5, and 6 verbally across disciplines.
- **No single prior source unifies all six** with the exact "regulator scalarizes a hidden value vector via a weighted proxy, hidden harm in unmeasured directions" formalism. The novelty that survives is narrow: an explicit *set-monotonicity* statement (piece 3) and welding the *sufficiency* condition (piece 4) and set-monotonicity onto the regulator-scalarization framing in one integrated model.
- **The strongest exact matches are Baker 2002 / Feltham-Xie 1994 for piece 2** (distortion = non-proportionality of the proxy-sensitivity and value-gradient vectors) and **Reynaert-Sallee 2016 for piece 5** (corrective policy gamed via a proxy, named "Goodhart's Law"). The framework should cite these as near-identical antecedents, not as adjacent work.

## Key Findings

The framework's claimed contribution is *synthesis*. The decisive question is therefore not whether each piece has a prior-art home (they all do) but whether the connections between pieces are already drawn in one place. They substantially are. The multitask principal-agent (LEN) literature is where the "proxy-vector-with-weights, hidden harm in unmeasured directions" formalism actually lives, and it already binds together least-cost effort allocation, the proportionality/congruity condition, the sufficiency/aggregation condition, and the comparative static that stronger incentives intensify distortion. An adversarial reading of the novelty claim therefore fails on the strong form ("nobody connected these") while surviving on a much weaker form ("nobody stated the set-monotonicity result or combined sufficiency + set-monotonicity in the regulator framing").

## Details — Piece-by-Piece Prior Art

### Piece 1 — Least-cost gaming / equimarginal load (load on each channel ∝ κ_j·w_j)
- **Strongest source:** Holmström & Milgrom (1991), "Multitask Principal–Agent Analyses: Incentive Contracts, Asset Ownership, and Job Design," *Journal of Law, Economics, & Organization* 7(special issue):24–52; with the explicit closed form in the LEN model (Feltham-Xie 1994, p. 433; Datar-Kulp-Lambert 2001).
- **Classification: SPECIAL CASE of the framework's claim; EXACT for the mechanism.** In the standard quadratic-cost LEN model the agent's first-order condition yields effort on each task proportional to the marginal-cost-weighted incentive coefficient (e\* = (w/2)·C⁻¹b, collapsing to e\* = w·b when the cost matrix C = ½I). This is the framework's equimarginal "load ∝ κ_j·w_j" claim under a different label. The mechanism is identical; the framework's contribution here is purely relabeling.

### Piece 2 — Distortion / congruity (hidden harm ∝ weighted average of exchange rates h_j/w_j; zero iff h_j = c·w_j)
- **Strongest source:** Baker (2002), "Distortion and Risk in Optimal Incentive Contracts," *Journal of Human Resources* 37(4):728–751; co-equal with Feltham & Xie (1994), "Performance Measure Congruity and Diversity in Multi-Task Principal/Agent Relations," *The Accounting Review* 69(3):429–453 (key results p. 433). Origin: Baker (1992), *JPE* 100(3):598–614.
- **Classification: EXACT MATCH.** Baker's abstract states he uses "a multi-task model to develop a two-parameter characterization of performance measures" — the two parameters being **distortion and risk**. Distortion is defined via the cosine of the angle between the vector of marginal products of effort on the performance measure and the vector of marginal products on firm value; distortion is zero **if and only if** the two vectors are proportional/collinear (b = γβ). This is precisely the framework's "zero distortion iff h_j = c·w_j (proportionality/congruity)." Feltham-Xie's congruity is the same proportionality condition, with optimal weights s⁰ = (Y′Y)⁻¹Y′d and a congruity index equal to the squared cosine between the value-gradient d and the achievable sensitivity vector. **This is the single closest exact match to any framework piece — the framework's piece 2 is a restatement of Baker/Feltham-Xie.**

### Piece 3 — Set-monotonicity (adding/removing a channel can move harm either way; count is not the governing variable)
- **Strongest source:** Datar, Kulp & Lambert (2001), "Balancing Performance Measures," *Journal of Accounting Research* 39(1):75–92; verbally supported by John et al. (2023) on nested/multi-proxy structure.
- **Classification: ADJACENT / partial SPECIAL CASE.** DKL show that an increase in a measure's sensitivity does not necessarily increase its optimal weight, even when the measure is perfectly congruent — i.e., adding measurement information has non-monotone effects on the optimal contract. This is the closest analytic antecedent, but no prior source states a clean theorem that "channel count is not the governing variable for *harm*." **This is the framework's thinnest prior-art overlap and its most plausibly novel formal statement.**

### Piece 4 — Sufficiency (when is Σ w_j P_j a sufficient statistic / lossless aggregation?)
- **Strongest source:** Banker & Datar (1989), "Sensitivity, Precision, and Linear Aggregation of Signals for Performance Evaluation," *Journal of Accounting Research* 27(1):21–39; with Amershi, Banker & Datar (1990), "Economic Sufficiency and Statistical Sufficiency in Accounting Aggregation," *The Accounting Review* 65(1):113–130, and Şabac & Yoo (2018), "Performance Measure Aggregation in Multi-Task Agencies," *Contemporary Accounting Research* 35(2):716–733.
- **Classification: EXACT MATCH (for linear aggregation) / SPECIAL CASE.** Banker-Datar derive necessary and sufficient conditions on the joint density of signals under which a linear aggregate (weighted sum) is optimal, and characterize the relative weights. Şabac-Yoo show statistically sufficient aggregation requires at least as many measures as effective tasks; a single scalar is generally only *economically* (not *statistically*) sufficient. The framework's "when is the scalar a sufficient statistic for the vector P" question is exactly the Banker-Datar / Amershi-Banker-Datar / Şabac-Yoo question. Chetty (2009), "Sufficient Statistics for Welfare Analysis," supplies the public-finance "sufficient statistic for welfare" vocabulary but addresses a different object (reduced-form welfare elasticities) and is only ADJACENT.

### Piece 5 — Second best / corrective policy gamed (the named "Goodhart's law" in economics)
- **Strongest source:** Reynaert & Sallee (2016/2019), NBER WP 22911, "Corrective Policy and Goodhart's Law: The Case of Carbon Emissions from Automobiles" (published as "Who Benefits When Firms Game Corrective Policies?", *AEJ: Economic Policy*); foundationally Lipsey & Lancaster (1956), "The General Theory of Second Best," *Review of Economic Studies* 24(1):11–32; and Ito & Sallee (2018), "The Economics of Attribute-Based Regulation," *Review of Economics and Statistics* 100(2):319–336.
- **Classification: EXACT MATCH (Reynaert-Sallee) / foundational SPECIAL CASE (Lipsey-Lancaster).** Reynaert-Sallee explicitly model a regulator using an imperfect proxy (the fuel-economy rating) that firms game, and invoke Goodhart's Law by name — they credit Meredith Fowlie "for introducing us to the term Goodhart's Law in this policy context" and define it as "the notion that strategic actions will alter the meaning of a measure once stakes are introduced." Their central result is verbatim: "Combined with Goodhart's Law, this means that strengthening corrective policy will exacerbate choice distortions and wasteful gaming, which together lower the social value created in the market." That is exactly the framework's piece 5 ("perfecting one measured margin while another stays unmeasured can reduce welfare; corrective policy on a proxy gets gamed"). Lipsey-Lancaster is the general theorem that perfecting one Paretian condition while another is constrained is, in general, no longer desirable — the welfare-theoretic foundation of the same point.

### Piece 6 — Selection pressure → harm magnitude (stronger competition/selection intensifies distortion)
- **Strongest source:** Holmström & Milgrom (1991) for the incentive-strength channel; Frankel & Kartik (2019), "Muddled Information," *Journal of Political Economy* 127(4):1739–1776, for the stakes channel; Braganza (2022), "Proxyeconomics," *Royal Society Open Science* 9(2):211030, and John et al. (2023) for the selection/competition channel.
- **Classification: EXACT MATCH (over-determined).** HM 1991: with substitute tasks, raising the incentive weight on a measured task diverts effort from unmeasured tasks (the basis for muting incentives). Frankel-Kartik: "As incentives to take higher actions increase—due to higher stakes or more manipulable signaling technology—more information is revealed about gaming ability, and less about natural actions." Braganza/John et al.: proxy-based selection pressure pushes the proxy away from the goal. The "stronger pressure → more distortion" claim appears in at least three independent literatures.

## THE KEY VERDICT — Does any single source unify ≥2 pieces?

**Yes. Three distinct unifiers exist, each connecting multiple pieces inside one model or framework.**

**1. The multitask LEN lineage (Holmström-Milgrom 1991 → Feltham-Xie 1994 → Baker 2002 → Datar-Kulp-Lambert 2001) connects pieces 1, 2, 4, and 6 in one formalism.** This is the most damaging finding for the novelty claim. Within a single LEN model: (a) the agent's cost-minimizing effort allocation is proportional to marginal-cost-weighted incentive weights [piece 1]; (b) distortion is exactly non-proportionality between the proxy-sensitivity vector and the value-gradient [piece 2]; (c) optimal weights and the conditions for lossless linear aggregation are derived [piece 4, via Banker-Datar and Şabac-Yoo in the same tradition]; and (d) higher-powered incentives on a measured task draw effort away from unmeasured tasks, increasing hidden harm [piece 6]. Pieces 1, 2, and 6 co-occur explicitly in Holmström-Milgrom 1991 and Baker 2002 (Baker's distortion-risk trade-off attenuates the optimal piece rate by the distortion cosine, and substitute tasks make stronger incentives more harmful). Piece 4 is connected through the same multitask aggregation formalism. **This literature is a genuine pre-existing unifier of four of the six pieces.**

**2. Reynaert-Sallee (2016) connects pieces 1, 5, and 6 in one structural model.** A regulator uses a proxy (fuel-economy rating); firms game it by reallocating along measured vs. unmeasured attributes [piece 1]; perfecting the corrective margin is gamed and can reduce welfare [piece 5]; and strengthening the policy intensifies distortion and wasteful gaming [piece 6] — all with Goodhart's Law named explicitly. Ito-Sallee (2018) extends the same regulator-gaming structure to the multidimensional (attribute-by-weight) case.

**3. John-Caldwell-McCoy-Braganza (2023), "Dead rats, dopamine, performance metrics, and peacock tails: Proxy failure is an inherent risk in goal-oriented systems," *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 47:e67 (published online 26 June 2023, DOI 10.1017/S0140525X23002753), connects pieces 3, 5, and 6 verbally across disciplines.** It sets up exactly the framework's primitives, framed "in terms of a regulator with a goal, who uses a proxy to incentivize or select agents in service of this goal," and proposes "such a unifying mechanism, which we label proxy failure." Its core proposition is verbatim: "when the pursuit of a goal depends on the optimization of an imperfect proxy in a complex system, a pressure emerges that pushes the proxy away from the goal" [piece 6]. It identifies this with the economics "Goodhart's law" gamed-proxy result [piece 5] and discusses nested/multi-proxy hierarchies where adding proxies has ambiguous effects [piece 3]. It is a verbal/structural unifier, not a single closed-form model, but it explicitly claims to be a unifying mechanism across disciplines.

**Conclusion on the verdict:** The framework's synthesis is NOT novel at the level of "nobody has connected these." The multitask LEN lineage already connects four of the six pieces in one formalism, and two further sources connect overlapping subsets. What appears genuinely novel is (i) an explicit *set-monotonicity* theorem [piece 3] as a stated result rather than an implication, and (ii) welding the *sufficiency* condition [piece 4] and set-monotonicity [piece 3] onto the regulator-scalarization framing in one integrated model. The contribution should be reframed from "unifying separate, unconnected results that economics, accounting, and management proved separately and never connected" to "extending the multitask-distortion model with an explicit set-monotonicity and sufficiency treatment and porting it to the regulator/selection setting."

## "Should-be-cited but not on the candidate list" — additional prior art surfaced

- **Baker (1992), "Incentive Contracts and Performance Measurement," *Journal of Political Economy* 100(3):598–614.** The original statement that a measure is undistorted iff its sensitivity is collinear with the value-gradient. Foundational to piece 2; predates Baker 2002.
- **Amershi, Banker & Datar (1990), "Economic Sufficiency and Statistical Sufficiency in Accounting Aggregation," *The Accounting Review* 65(1):113–130.** Directly distinguishes economic vs. statistical sufficiency of aggregated signals — central to piece 4 and a closer match than Chetty 2009.
- **Surrogation literature: Choi, Hecht & Tayler (2012/2013), "Strategy Selection, Surrogation, and Strategic Performance Measurement Systems," *Journal of Accounting Research* 51(1):105–133.** Documents proxy-target substitution and shows single-measure compensation worsens it relative to multiple measures — a measurement-theoretic complement to pieces 2 and 3 that the framework omits, and the management-accounting analogue of "proxy becomes the target."
- **Bevan & Hood (2006), "What's Measured Is What Matters: Targets and Gaming in the English Public Health Care System," *Public Administration* 84(3):517–538;** and **Courty & Marschke (2004), "An Empirical Investigation of Gaming Responses to Explicit Performance Incentives," *Journal of Labor Economics* 22(1):23–56.** The empirical public-management home for piece 5 (gaming of targets). Courty-Marschke also supply an operational gaming test (correlation of measure with the true goal falls after the measure is activated).
- **Kerr (1975), "On the Folly of Rewarding A While Hoping for B," *Academy of Management Journal*;** and **Ridgway (1956), "Dysfunctional Consequences of Performance Measurements," *Administrative Science Quarterly*.** The pre-formal management statements of the whole phenomenon — should be cited as intellectual priors.
- **Manheim & Garrabrant (2018), "Categorizing Variants of Goodhart's Law" (arXiv:1803.04585).** The AI-safety taxonomy (regressional, extremal, causal, adversarial Goodhart) is the closest existing *taxonomy* and should be positioned explicitly against the framework's set-monotonicity claim, since both partition Goodhart failure modes.
- **Holmström (2017 Nobel lecture), "Pay for Performance and Beyond," *American Economic Review* 107(7):1753–1777.** The authoritative retrospective tying the informativeness principle, multitasking, and incentive-muting together — itself a near-survey-level unifier of pieces 1, 2, and 6.
- **Prendergast (1999, *JEL* 37(1):7–63) and Gibbons (1998, *JEP*):** surveys that *discuss* pieces 1, 2, and 6 in adjacent sections (Prendergast explicitly attributes "multi-tasking" distortion to Holmström-Milgrom 1991) but do NOT structurally unify them in one model. They corroborate that the pieces co-habit one subfield (personnel/organizational economics) rather than living in separate, unconnected literatures — which itself weakens the "never connected" claim.

## Recommendations
1. **Reframe the novelty claim immediately (stage 1, before any other revision).** Drop "brings under one formalism results that economics, accounting, and management proved separately and never connected." Replace with "extends the multitask-distortion (LEN) model with an explicit set-monotonicity theorem and a sufficiency condition, and ports it to the regulator/selection setting." *Threshold that changes this:* if you cannot produce a set-monotonicity theorem or a sufficiency result that is absent from Banker-Datar / DKL, the surviving novelty collapses and the work becomes a re-exposition.
2. **Cite Baker 2002 and Feltham-Xie 1994 as the direct antecedent for piece 2**, stating explicitly that the framework's congruity condition (h_j = c·w_j) is their proportionality/collinearity condition restated. Cite Baker 1992 as the origin.
3. **Rest piece 4 on Banker-Datar 1989, Amershi-Banker-Datar 1990, and Şabac-Yoo 2018**, not on Chetty 2009 (different object). State whether your sufficiency condition adds anything to Şabac-Yoo's "≥ as many measures as effective tasks" result; if it does not, concede the point.
4. **Cite Reynaert-Sallee 2016 and Lipsey-Lancaster 1956 as direct antecedents for piece 5**, and Ito-Sallee 2018 for the multidimensional attribute-based-regulation case (the closest existing regulator-side analogue of your weighted-proxy setup).
5. **Concentrate the original-contribution argument on piece 3 (set-monotonicity) and the integrated piece-3+4 treatment within the regulator framing.** *Threshold for defensible novelty:* a stated set-monotonicity theorem with an explicit counterexample showing harm moving in both directions as channel count changes — DKL 2001 only shows non-monotone *weights*, not non-monotone *harm-vs-count*, so this is the gap to claim.
6. **Add the surrogation literature (Choi-Hecht-Tayler), the public-management gaming literature (Bevan-Hood, Courty-Marschke), and the AI-safety taxonomy (Manheim-Garrabrant) to the citation set** to pre-empt the objection that you ignored the empirical, behavioral, and taxonomic homes of the phenomenon.

## Caveats
- Exact equation/page labels for Baker 2002 and Datar-Kulp-Lambert 2001 were verified through high-fidelity peer-reviewed restatements (Budde 2007, *JAR* 45(3); Schnedler 2006, IZA DP 2124) plus Baker's own abstract and verbatim snippet fragments, not from the paywalled original page images. The Feltham-Xie optimal-weight result is reliably at p. 433. The cosine/proportionality characterization of Baker's distortion is corroborated by multiple independent sources, but the original endnote numbering should be confirmed against the JHR original.
- "Unify" is interpreted as structurally connecting pieces in one model. The proxy-failure BBS article (John et al. 2023) is a verbal/structural unifier across disciplines, not a single closed-form model; a referee who requires a formal model may discount it as a unifier while still treating it as prior art for the regulator-proxy-agent framing. The LEN lineage and Reynaert-Sallee are formal-model unifiers and are not subject to this discount.
- The framework's notation (κ_j marginal costs, h_j/w_j "exchange rates") was matched to the literature's marginal-cost-matrix / sensitivity-vector / value-gradient notation by structural correspondence. If the framework defines "harm" as a stock in unmeasured value dimensions rather than a flow effort-misallocation, the match to Baker/Feltham-Xie remains structural but the welfare accounting differs — this is worth stating precisely in the paper to avoid an inflated novelty rebuttal.
- This investigation exhausted its web-search budget after 18 queries. Two candidate-unifier surveys (Gibbons 1998 *JEP*; Bénabou-Tirole 2016 on bonus culture) were confirmed only at the abstract/secondary level rather than full-text; both are well-established as discussing-but-not-formally-unifying sources, but a full-text check of Bénabou-Tirole 2016 is the one residual gap if the framework's selection-pressure channel (piece 6) is meant to operate through competitive signaling rather than principal-agent incentives.
