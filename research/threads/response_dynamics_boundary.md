# Response dynamics boundary

Iteration: 36

Status: modeling boundary note for Q11 and Q14. This note adds a small
executable toy for stochastic observation and endogenous stakes. It does not
prove a general convergence result, a performative fixed-point theorem, or a
policy optimum.

## Approach boundary

Three possible approaches were live at the start of this pass.

Proof-note first. A theorem-first pass would be premature. Iteration 35 already
states the deterministic capacity boundary under fixed `M`, `d`, `V`, weights,
separable quadratic costs, and deterministic observation. The new question
changes the update rules: observation can be noisy, stakes can respond to
observed harm, and agents/regulators can forecast different harm signals. There
is no single theorem target until those dynamics are declared.

Review-only first. A prose-only review would preserve the non-license but would
not close the model gap. The excluded claims are dynamic claims, so the next
piece of evidence should include at least one falsifiable update rule.

Tiny-simulation first. A small executable toy is the right first pass because it
can keep claims narrow while testing whether the static capacity story survives
declared dynamic updates. The simulation is not evidence for real domains; it is
a check that stochastic observation and endogenous stakes can break or alter the
deterministic static story before any theorem or policy prose is attempted.

Chosen approach: hybrid. This note records the boundary and
`research/simulations/iteration36_response_dynamics.py` supplies two seeded
checks.

## Toy 1: stochastic observation of response channels

The deterministic Iteration 35 rule observes the current best-response action
and hardens a largest-action channel. The dynamic toy changes only the
observation step:

`observed_action_j = true_action_j + epsilon_j`,

with seeded Gaussian observation error. The regulator hardens the channel with
largest observed action for a fixed repair budget.

The toy uses unequal score weights. Channel 1 has most of the score capacity,
so one oracle hardening step would push
`S(M) < d^2/(2V)`. Under the seeded noisy observation, the regulator hardens a
low-capacity channel instead, leaving `S(M)` above the no-gaming threshold after
the fixed budget.

Licensed statement: stochastic observation can make a declared largest-observed
action repair fail within a fixed budget even when an oracle update would cross
the static capacity threshold.

Non-license: this is not a theorem that noisy repair usually fails, not a
cycling claim, and not a claim about optimal filtering. It only shows that the
deterministic capacity proof does not transfer without an observation model and
repair-budget condition.

## Toy 2: endogenous stakes closure

The static toy treats stakes `V` as fixed. The dynamic toy declares the
recurrence

`V_{t+1} = (1-lambda) V_t + lambda V0 exp(-rho H_obs_t)`.

Agents respond myopically to current `V_t`; the regulator updates future stakes
from observed harm `H_obs_t`. The hidden harm weights are fixed. The two paths
change only the observability of that harm:

- visible-harm path: `H_obs_t` equals the hidden harm generated by the action;
- invisible-harm path: `H_obs_t = 0` even though hidden harm is generated.

Under the declared recurrence, the visible path lowers `V` enough to interrupt
gaming in later rounds, while the invisible path keeps `V` high and gaming
continues through the fixed horizon.

Licensed statement: endogenous-stakes conclusions depend on the declared
observed-harm signal and update rule. The same static score capacity can produce
different stakes paths when hidden harm is or is not observable.

Non-license: no performative fixed-point theorem follows. The recurrence is one
toy closure, not a general model of trust, institutional demand, strategic
forecasting, or policy optimality.

## Resulting boundary

Iteration 36 licenses only this narrow dynamic statement:

Declared response dynamics can break or alter the static capacity story. In
particular, noisy observation can miss the repair channel within a fixed budget,
and endogenous stakes can follow different paths depending on what harm is
observable under the declared recurrence.

The corresponding non-license is:

Do not claim general convergence, persistent cycling, performative stability,
endogenous-stakes optimality, or policy optimality from these toys. Any later
dynamic result must declare the observation process, repair budget, harm signal,
forecast rule, stakes recurrence, and agent response rule before importing the
Iteration 35 capacity threshold.
