# Thread: response-modeling contract for Goodhart claims

Iteration type: `edit`

## 1. Motivation

Iterations 10-13 narrowed the project from universal claims about the shape of
Goodhart pressure to conditional claims about response channels. Fixed charges
produce lumpy regimes rather than universal one-channel drift. Selection versus
intervention is a causal distinction relative to a declared type/action
representation, not an observable property of marginal laws alone. Convex
intervention bounds exist only after declaring action spaces, costs, stakes, and
hidden harm functionals.

The useful next object is therefore not another slogan about Goodhart's law. It
is a contract for making a Goodhart claim complete enough to evaluate.

## 2. Current contract

A Goodhart claim is incomplete unless it declares the response model. The
minimum contract is:

1. **Type representation.** What is the type space `U`, what is the baseline law
   `nu`, and why are those features treated as fixed rather than future
   responses to the policy?
2. **State and baseline behavior.** What observed state `s in S` is generated by
   baseline kernel `K_0(ds | u)`?
3. **Policy exposure.** What policy, score, threshold, prize, or feedback
   variable `theta` creates optimization pressure, and who observes it?
4. **Response channel.** Does `theta` change only participation weights
   `W_theta(u)`, or does it change the fixed-type response kernel
   `K_theta(ds | u)`?
5. **Action geometry.** If fixed-type behavior changes, what action space,
   feasible movement, caps, fixed charges, costs, or search prior makes some
   moves available and others unavailable?
6. **Proxy and target.** What proxy is optimized, what target or hidden welfare
   quantity is protected, and what residual relation `P approx phi(G)` is being
   tested?
7. **Aggregation rule.** If the proxy is multidimensional, how are components
   combined: additive, weighted, conjunctive, Pareto, lexicographic, or
   something else?
8. **Evidence standard.** What observation would distinguish reweighting of
   fixed types from fixed-type behavior change, and what observation would
   falsify the proposed action/cost geometry?

Claim [tentative]: this contract is the right methodological object for the
current project. Proxy pressure alone does not determine Goodhart behavior. The
response model determines which mathematical result is relevant: a selection
response curve, a reweighting bound, a convex or nonconvex intervention budget,
an aggregation/exchange-rate calculation, or a response-shape prediction.

Toy example: a school test-score policy is not yet a Goodhart model. It becomes
one only after saying whether schools are fixed types being selected for
funding, whether the same schools change teaching at fixed type, whether
test-prep effort has caps or fixed activation costs, how test score aggregates
skills, and what hidden target distinguishes learning from test-format drilling.

## 3. Why this is not just generic causal modeling

The contract is causal-modeling discipline, but it is specialized to proxy
optimization. The organizing question is always:

`policy pressure on P -> response channel -> divergence between P and protected target H/G`.

A generic causal model may ask any counterfactual question. A Goodhart response
model asks which response to proxy pressure creates target-relevant distortion,
and which evidence would support that mechanism over nearby alternatives.

Claim [tentative]: the stable core of the project is not "Goodhart has one
shape." It is "Goodhart claims must route proxy pressure through a declared
response channel before making shape, welfare, or evidence claims."

Counterexample attempt: this could become vacuous if every application merely
says "geometry matters." To avoid that, each contract must name the downstream
calculation it licenses. For pure selection, this should be a baseline response
curve or reweighting bound. For intervention, it should be an action/cost/search
model and a hidden harm functional. For response shape, it should name a
pre-specified shape measure such as support, rank, description length, or
legibility.

## 4. Representation relativity

The response-kernel distinction remains:

`mu_theta(A) =
  integral W_theta(u) K_theta(A | u) nu(du) /
  integral W_theta(u) nu(du)`.

Pure selection relative to `(U, K_0)` means policy dependence enters through
`W_theta` while `K_theta = K_0` for `nu`-almost every type. Intervention means
`K_theta != K_0` on a positive-mass set of types.

This is not representation-free. A too-rich `U` can encode the whole future
policy-contingent response plan and turn intervention into selection over
types. A too-coarse `U` can make stable heterogeneity look like behavior change.
The contract therefore requires a substantive defense of `U`: why these
attributes are fixed for the comparison, and why the omitted variables are
actions, noise, or outcomes rather than type.

Claim [tentative]: declaring `U` is not a nuisance caveat; it is the point at
which the empirical claim enters. A school, hospital, model checkpoint, worker,
or institution can be "fixed type" under one question and "state after prior
adaptation" under another. The same marginal distribution does not settle this.

Toy example: if the same hospital changes coding behavior after a ranking
formula is announced, repeated-hospital observations support a fixed-type
kernel change. If instead only hospitals with already-high coding intensity
enter the ranked population, a selection model may be adequate. Cross-sectional
post-policy outcomes alone usually cannot decide.

## 5. Evidence standards

The contract implies different evidence by channel.

For pure selection, relevant evidence includes the baseline joint distribution,
tail response curves `E[H | P >= t] - E[H]`, weighting rules, and the size of
the reweighting budget. Repeated observations of fixed types are helpful but not
always necessary if the claim is explicitly about selecting from a fixed pool.

For intervention, marginal shifts are not enough. Credible evidence should come
from at least one of:

- repeated observations of the same type before and after policy exposure;
- randomized or staggered exposure to the metric;
- action traces showing which feasible moves were taken;
- exogenous variation in costs, caps, stakes, or aggregation rules;
- a defended structural model of the response process.

For welfare claims, evidence must also identify the hidden target or harm
functional. Proxy-only manipulation can be an intervention for proxy validity
without increasing hidden harm. Conversely, high-harm movement can be invisible
if the proxy and target are not separately measured.

## 6. What this licenses

The response-modeling contract licenses:

- rejecting Goodhart claims made from outcome distributions alone when the
  selection/intervention distinction is load-bearing;
- replacing universal shape claims with conditional response-regime claims;
- treating selection bounds, intervention bounds, aggregation results, and
  response-shape predictions as sibling outputs of different contracts;
- asking empirical applications to pre-state type, action, cost, aggregation,
  and hidden welfare mappings.

It does not license:

- a universal theorem that proxy pressure has one characteristic shape;
- an intrinsic selection/intervention classification from `mu_theta` alone;
- a welfare conclusion from proxy movement without a hidden harm model;
- post-hoc choice of type space, action coordinates, or complexity measure after
  seeing the failure.

## 7. Next branches

The contract turns the project into four more precise workstreams:

1. **Application evidence standards.** Write domain-specific mappings that state
   `U`, `W_theta`, `K_theta`, actions, costs, aggregation, and falsifiers.
2. **Intervention bounds conditional on action geometry.** Extend Iteration 13
   to stochastic response, endogenous stakes, and nonconvex fixed-charge/capped
   costs.
3. **Response-geometry taxonomy.** Keep refining the menu of shape predictions:
   quadratic, fixed-charge, capped, low-rank, and search-prior regimes.
4. **Empirical/ML mapping discipline.** For ML and institutional cases, do not
   call a case selection or intervention without identifying the analogue of
   type, action, cost, feasible movement, policy exposure, and hidden welfare.
